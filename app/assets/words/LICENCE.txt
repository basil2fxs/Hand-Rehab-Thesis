Syllables word bank: licence and provenance
===========================================

Files
-----
syllables_source.txt   the word list in source form, one line per word
syllables_bank.json    the built bank the game loads
                       (scripts/build_syllables_bank.py makes it)

Licence
-------
Both files were written for this project (Basil Toufexis, Curtin
University thesis project, 2026) and carry the same licence as the
rest of this repository. They may be used, changed and redistributed
with it.

Provenance
----------
Every word, syllable split, stress mark and band was written by hand
against ordinary Australian English usage. No third-party word list,
dictionary, corpus file or database was copied, in whole or in part.
In particular:

- The Oxford Wordlist (Oxford University Press Australia, the 500
  most frequent words in the writing of Australian children in their
  first three years of school) is copyright OUP. It was not consulted
  as data and none of it is reproduced here. The idea it stands for,
  that a children's bank should be built on words children actually
  use, is not copyrightable and is what the hand list aims at.
- The Macquarie Dictionary's syllable-division convention is used as
  a RULE (cut at the spelling boundary nearest the spoken one:
  rab-bit, ti-ger), which is a convention anyone may follow. No
  Macquarie entry, split or pronunciation was copied.
- No frequency database (SUBTLEX-UK, CPB-Lex), pronouncing dictionary
  (cmudict) or hyphenation file (Moby Hyphenator II) was downloaded,
  parsed or shipped.

Band assignment (A everyday, B less often met, C rare, plus every
four-syllable word) is a judgement made by hand to the rule in the
brief, not a copied frequency figure.

The bigger bank, if it is ever wanted
-------------------------------------
The research note for this mode sets out a route to a larger,
frequency-graded bank from open resources. Anyone taking it must
download each source themselves, keep it out of this repository, and
carry its terms:

  CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (cmudict), BSD licence: phonemes with
    stress digits, so syllable count and primary stress come free.
  Moby Hyphenator II (Grady Ward, Moby Project, public domain,
    Project Gutenberg ebook 3204): written hyphenation points for
    187,175 words.
  Kuperman, Stadthagen-Gonzalez and Brysbaert (2012) age-of-
    acquisition ratings: which words children know by 6 to 8.
  SUBTLEX-UK (van Heuven, Mandera, Keuleers and Brysbaert 2014) or
    CPB-Lex (Green, Keogh, Sun and O'Brien 2023): child-facing
    frequencies. Confirm redistribution terms before shipping any
    derived file.
  Wiktionary via wiktextract: CC BY-SA and GFDL. Using it makes the
    derived bank share-alike, so the note recommends sending
    Wiktionary disagreements to a review list instead of into the
    bank.

Splits from any of those sources still need a human pass: English
syllable division in print is a convention rather than a fact (Kearns
2020, Reading Research Quarterly, found the two taught rules fit 70.6
and 30.5 percent of applicable words), and the spoken syllable and the
printed chunk must agree exactly or the game teaches the wrong pair.

Review
------
The reviewer field on every entry is empty. Before the bank is used
with children, a teacher or speech pathologist should check a random
sample for split, stress and suitability, and their initials go in
that field. The thesis methods section should state who reviewed what.
