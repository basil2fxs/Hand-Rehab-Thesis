FORCE UNITS
===========

Written 2026-09-03. What the newton figures in this software mean,
where the constant comes from, and how accurate they are.

The pads
--------
SingleTact CS8-10N: calibrated 8 mm diameter, 10 N full scale. The
calibration is stored on the matched electronics; the Arduino
firmware (arduino/, read only) reads the sensor's 10-bit output over
I2C every 5 ms and forwards it untouched as FSR: v1,v2,v3,v4.

The manual's register map puts the sensor output at 10 bits, 0 to
0x3FF for 0 to 2 V, with the working range 0x100 to 0x2FF (0.5 to
1.5 V), so zero force sits near 256 counts and full scale near 767.
This matches the resting readings the rig has always shown (about
235 to 260 counts unloaded; the sub-256 readings are slight tension
in the pad mounting). The conversion the manual gives is

    Load (N) = (counts - baseline counts) / 512 counts x rating (N)

so for a 10 N part one count is 10 / 512 = 0.01953 N, or 51.2
counts per newton. That is config fsr.force_calibration_n_per_count
(with fsr.sensor_rating_n and fsr.counts_full_scale beside it so the
three cannot drift apart; a test pins the ratio).

An earlier version of the analysis notebook and of the fsr comment
in config/default.yaml assumed a 45 N part (0.0879 N per count),
which put every newton figure 4.5 times too high. Rayan's flat
"51.2 counts per newton" was the 10 N figure all along.

What is baseline-subtracted
---------------------------
The constant is a slope only. The baseline term in the manual's
equation is the detector's tracked resting level: an EMA with a
time constant near 10 s at 200 Hz, primed from the calibration
profile's resting capture and frozen at each press's rising edge.
So the sensor's 256-count zero offset, the pad's chassis preload and
the finger's resting load are all in the baseline and none of them
reach a newton figure. This is the manual's "tare before measuring",
done continuously instead of once.

The raw fsr1 to fsr8 columns in raw.csv stay raw counts, offset
included, so the notebook can re-tare its own way. peak_force_n and
impulse_n in trials.csv, and peak_force_mean and impulse_mean in the
block summary, are in newtons (and newton-seconds) whenever the
constant is configured; block_summary.force_unit says "N". Sessions
recorded before September 2026 have no constant and read "counts";
the notebook converts those on the 10 N fallback and every later
session on its own config_snapshot.

Percent-of-max targets (Force Pilot, the quick calibration's light
and max presses, the press thresholds) stay in counts. They are
ratios of counts to counts on the same pad, so the constant cancels
and nothing about them changed.

Accuracy: the honest sentence for the thesis
--------------------------------------------
Force is reported in newtons from the SingleTact CS8-10N factory
calibration (512 counts per 10 N, 19.5 mN per count) after a
per-session software tare that subtracts each pad's tracked resting
level. The manufacturer specifies linearity under 2 percent,
hysteresis under 4 percent and drift up to 4 percent of full scale
over ten minutes at half load, so absolute force is accurate to
about +/- 0.4 N and within-session comparisons to about +/- 0.2 N; a
bench check with 100 g to 1 kg masses (scripts/force_check.py, date
to be filled in) found a slope of <x> counts per newton, <y> percent
from the nominal value.

Fill in <x>, <y> and the date from the JSON the script writes to
config/calibration/force_check_<date>.json. Until the check has been
run the sentence stops at "about +/- 0.2 N" and says the bench check
is pending; do not quote a slope that was not measured.

Two things the sentence does not cover, said plainly:
- Resolution. One count is 0.02 N, and resting noise is about 1.1
  counts SD, so a single sample resolves about 0.02 N and a 2 s mean
  about 0.002 N. The sensor updates at 50 to 120 Hz while the board
  polls at 200 Hz, so consecutive identical counts are normal.
- Drift during a hold. At 2 percent of full scale per minute, a 5 s
  hold can drift 0.02 N, one count; the games report nothing finer
  than that. Longer holds are re-tared between trials by the force
  view's rebaseline.

The bench check
---------------
scripts/force_check.py puts known masses (100, 200, 500, 1000 g by
default, through a rigid puck no wider than the pad) on each pad,
tares over 5 s, averages 2 s per mass ascending then descending, and
holds 500 g for 60 s. Expected counts above tare at g = 9.81:

    mass     newtons   10 N part   45 N part
    100 g     0.98        50.2        11.2
    200 g     1.96       100.4        22.3
    500 g     4.91       251.1        55.8
   1000 g     9.81       502.3       111.6

Acceptance: each point within 20 counts of expected, slope within 2
percent of 51.2 counts per newton, ascending against descending at
500 g within 20 counts, drift over the hold at most 10 counts,
return to tare within 10 counts. A slope near 11.4 counts per
newton is reported as a part mismatch (a 45 N part), not a failure.
--write-slope --config-out <path> writes the fitted constant into
the yaml named; the script never writes config/user_settings.yaml
or anything under sessions/.

Sources
-------
SingleTact user manual (register map, 10-bit output, the load
equation and the tare advice) and the CS8-10N product page
(linearity, repeatability, hysteresis, drift, update rate, overload),
both read 2026-09-03 and listed in the September research notes.
Demouche 2025 (healthy fingertip force on the 2025 button device) is
the comparison the fsr threshold comment now reads against: the
pinky trigger of 46 counts is 0.90 N, a third of the 2.66 N healthy
little-finger mean.
