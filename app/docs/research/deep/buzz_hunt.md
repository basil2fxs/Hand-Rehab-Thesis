### 1.2 The motor path and its timing

- The host sends `STIM:n`; the firmware drives the pin at its fixed PWM duty and turns it off on its own 150 ms timer (`Motor.cpp` `triggerStimMotor`, `updateStimMotors`; `Config.cpp` 22). Duty is 200 of 255 (78 percent) on index, middle and ring, and 240 of 255 (94 percent) on the little finger (`Config.cpp` 17-20; arithmetic). `pulse_motor` also schedules a scoped STOP at 150 ms, which lands after the firmware's own stop (`engine.py` 7631-7676). A STIM for another finger on the same board inside a live hold writes a STOP first (`_send_stim` 7422-7478), which is why span pulses are 400 ms apart.
- Measured on the study MacBook on 24 September 2026 with the laptop microphone (`finger_rehab/audio/latency_measure.py`, averaged envelope over ten pulses per motor, onset at 5 SD above the averaged noise): motor sound 105.7, 102.5, 111.6 and 106.2 ms after the command on channels 1 to 4, less a 31.5 ms microphone delay, that is 74.2, 71.0, 80.1 and 74.7 ms (arithmetic; `app/config/latency_profile.yaml`). The saved value is the median, 74 ms. When the pad is first felt is not measured.
- The design notes add that the motor reaches half strength about 100 to 140 ms after the command (`docs/research/new_modes/ranked.md` "Rank 3"); no data file for that figure is on disk.
- The docstrings and config quote "Precision Microdrives 310-103 class" values: lag about 40 ms, rise about 87 ms, stop about 115 ms. They disagree on what the 87 ms is: "rise to full amplitude" in `buzz_hunt.py` 51-52, `default.yaml` 1758-1760 and `engine.py` 7559-7560, but "87 ms to reach half amplitude" in `buzz_hunt.py` 268-269 and `rhythm.py` 98, and "rise to 50 percent (PMD AB-029)" in `default.yaml` 1763; `default.yaml` 1569-1571 and `echo.py` 130-132 give a third figure, full amplitude at about 130 ms, with no source. The hardware README's bill of materials lists only "Vibration motor on a motor PCB, with connector ... supplied by the Curtin electronics team, 2026"; the part number is not recorded anywhere in the repository.
- `scripts/latency_check.py` describes the phone-at-240-fps and piezo routes for lag, rise and stop; the docstring (`buzz_hunt.py` 96-101) says they have not been run.
- The four motors sit in four printed finger columns on one PLA base, fingertips curled down onto the pads (`hardware/README.md`; `hardware/images/chassis_section.png`; `app/docs/images/device.jpg`).
- Press timing floor (`docs/research/new_modes/modes-review.md`, "Device facts"): presses stamped 10 to 11 ms late on a bursting board, detector 7 to 11 ms after the raw crossing.

- The instruction lines are on the stage cards only (`buzz_hunt_screen.py` 53-66). Neither mentions speed. The card says "Hands flat on the pads" while the chassis has the fingertips curled down onto raised pads (`hardware/README.md`).
- The window, its level and any promotion are never shown. A timeout looks the same as a wrong answer: a dim dot.

## 2. Research questions and findings

### Q1. How accurate is healthy finger localisation of a suprathreshold stimulus, and is B1's 90 percent the right bar?

- Suprathreshold touch gives ceiling. A study that used the same suprathreshold touch on fingers and toes recorded no finger mislocalisation at all, in any participant; to obtain finger errors, Manser-Smith, Tame and Longo set von Frey hairs per person to about 70 percent correct (Cicmil et al. 2016 as reported in Manser-Smith et al. 2018, Experiment 2 Procedure [FT]; Cicmil's own abstract covers only the toes [ABS]).
- Weber et al. 2023 [FT, Methods, Results "Misreferrals"]: 33 healthy controls (19 to 63 years), a 6.1 Semmes-Weinstein monofilament of 100 gf (suprathreshold for every control), 90 stimulations per hand. Most controls made none, one or two misreferrals (a touch reported on another digit); two made them on 10 to 15 percent of trials. In controls and the uninjured hands of patients, 74 percent of misreferrals were confusions between the middle and ring fingers.
- Vibration on the answering fingers. Abrahamse et al. 2009 [FT, Method, Results]: 200 Hz vibration from miniature loudspeakers on the ring and index fingers of both hands, the same fingers answering on keys, white noise over headphones; entry required 95 percent accuracy on a random tactile block, and error percentages never exceeded 5 percent in any block. Their pilot work found that adjacent answering fingers raised tactile errors, which is why they used ring and index.
- Near threshold, accuracy falls to about half: 0.44 with the fingers together, 0.56 spread, 0.40 interwoven, with a 0.07 g von Frey hair (Overvliet et al. 2011, Table 1 [FT]); 0.89 on the thumb down to 0.52 on the little finger at a per-person threshold setting (Manser-Smith et al. 2018, Experiment 2 Results [FT]); Schweizer et al. 2000 used faint von Frey stimuli in eight people [ABS].
- Pilot: 13 of 13, 11 of 11, 5 of 8 (pilot). The 5 of 8 block's three errors were two timeouts and one little-to-ring press at 777 ms.
- Arithmetic: above 90 percent of 16 needs 15 or 16 correct. A person with a true accuracy of 0.97 clears it with probability 0.92, at 0.95 with 0.81 (binomial arithmetic). B1 is read on the cohort mean and its t interval, so one inattentive participant can turn it into "direction only".

Bearing: B1 should pass for an attentive cohort. Its misses will be mostly timeouts and motor slips rather than mislocalisations, and accuracy will not separate people.

### Q2. Do errors cluster on neighbouring fingers, and can B2 decide it at this n?

- Near threshold, yes. Mislocalisations fell more often on neighbours than on distant fingers (Schweizer et al. 2000 [ABS]; Schweizer et al. 2001 [ABS], where 20 hours of co-stimulating the thumb and little finger shifted the pattern). Braun et al. 2011 [ABS] tested near-threshold stimuli and favoured a central account in which finger representations overlap more as intensity falls; with 10, 20 and 100 Hz vibration, unambiguous mislocalisation effects appeared only at 10 Hz. Manser-Smith et al. 2018 [FT]: errors were biased towards the middle of the hand, index errors towards the little finger side (directionality index 0.20), ring errors towards the thumb side (-0.40), middle errors unbiased.
- Where sources disagree: Overvliet et al. 2011 [FT, Results "Mislocalization", Table 1] normalised each error type by the number of ways it could occur and found the neighbouring spot on the next fingertip was not chosen more often than any other spot on another fingertip; responses more than two positions away made up 0.20 to 0.33 of all responses.
- Suprathreshold, errors are few and fall between middle and ring (Weber et al. 2023 [FT]; Q1). Middle and ring are also the least individuated fingers in movement (Hager-Ross and Schieber 2000 [ABS]), so a slip onto the next pad is most likely there.
- Chance. The registered 0.5 assumes errors fall evenly on the four stimulated fingers. For a middle or ring target a random wrong finger is adjacent 2 times in 3. If three quarters of errors follow middle or ring stimulation, as Weber's 74 percent of middle-ring confusions implies at the least, a random wrong finger lands on a neighbour about 58 percent of the time (arithmetic).
- The gate. B2, B2d and B2-share count towards their minimum of 8 only the participants who made at least one wrong-finger error (code). A person makes at least one in 16 trials with probability 0.15, 0.28, 0.39, 0.56 and 0.82 at per-trial error rates of 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 percent, so 8 of 10 people clear it with probability under 0.001 at 1 and 2 percent, 0.010 at 3 percent, 0.11 at 5 percent and 0.72 at 10 percent (arithmetic, binomial). At healthy rates the three rows print "n under the design minimum".
- Power, if the gate counted everyone. At healthy misreferral rates of 1 to 2 percent, 10 people x 16 trials give 1.6 to 3.2 wrong-finger errors on average, and four or fewer 78 to 98 percent of the time (arithmetic, simulation). A one-sided binomial at 0.5 needs at least five errors, all adjacent (0.5 to the fifth power is 0.031; four all adjacent give 0.0625) (arithmetic). Simulated cohorts: with a true adjacent share of 0.8 to 0.9 and error rates of 2 to 3 percent, B2 passes 7 to 32 percent of the time; with errors that are random wrong fingers but follow middle or ring targets 74 percent of the time, the flat 0.5 null passes 1.4 to 9.0 percent as the error rate rises from 2 to 8 percent, against 0.5 to 2.8 percent for a null conditioned on the stimulated finger (simulation).
- Ties. The detector reports one sample's presses in lane order and Buzz Hunt takes the first queued press (`_respond_frame` 1842-1847), so two fingers crossing on the same 5 ms sample score for the lower lane. Reaction already corrects this bias with `_prefer_cued` (`reaction.py` 598-614); Buzz Hunt does not (code). When the finger pulled along is the lower neighbour, the tie scores as an adjacent error on the index side.

Bearing: as coded, B2 will almost always print "n under the design minimum", because the gate counts only people with an error; with the gate fixed it would still fail for lack of errors most of the time, whatever the hand does. Its chance line should condition on the stimulated finger and, as the design already says, it cannot show somatotopic misreferral.

### Q3. What does "press the finger that buzzed" measure?

- Pressing the finger that is vibrated is the classic case of perfect stimulus-response compatibility: with vibration on the answering fingers, Leonard 1959 found no RT difference between two, four and eight choices, a flat function (Proctor and Schneider 2018, section on S-R compatibility [FT]; ten Hoopen et al. 1982 qualify the result [META, as reported there]). Proctor and Schneider list pressing a key that vibrates under the finger among tasks that need no memory for the stimulus-response pairs [FT].
- Anema et al. 2008 [ABS]: three patients with finger agnosia pointed normally to the touched finger on their own hand but failed to show it on a hand drawing or to name it.

Bearing: localisation here is a sensorimotor response through the most direct route, close to a simple tactile reaction; it is not a finger identification (gnosis) test, and its errors mix perception with motor slips.

### Q4. What RT should a healthy hand show, and what does the rig add?

- Vibrotactile RT on the fingertips, 15 adults aged 21 to 37, 25 Hz, 300 micrometre, 40 ms pulses on the index and middle fingers (Mikkelsen et al. 2020, Table 1 [FT]): simple RT 215.45 ms (SD 51.13); two-choice RT, answered by the left or right mouse button, 427.60 ms (SD 93.81).
- Actuator and sound. Bao et al. 2019 timed RT, as the rig does, from the command to the actuator to the response (a thumb trigger), so the actuator's own start-up is inside it. Young adults responded in about 200 ms (head) to 230 ms (foot) with a C-2 tactor at 250 Hz; a coin-style ERM (200 Hz, response time under 91 ms) gave RTs 46 ms (young) and 42 ms (older) slower than the C-2 (under 33 ms); hearing the vibration shortened RTs at the finger by 8 ms (young) and 22 ms (older); RTs under 100 ms were discarded (Bao et al. 2019, Methods, Tables 1 and 2, Results Part II, Discussion [FT]).
- The rig. RT runs from the command frame (code). The motor is audible 71 to 80 ms after the command, 74 ms median (arithmetic from the profile); detector 7 to 11 ms; press stamps about 10 to 11 ms late (modes-review, "Device facts"). If the pad is felt when it is heard, the logged RT is the felt RT plus about 90 to 100 ms (arithmetic). Per finger the motor delay differs by up to 9 ms (arithmetic), so per-finger RT carries the motors as well as the fingers.
- Pilot medians 330, 510 and 497 ms (pilot), about 235 to 415 ms after the audible onset (arithmetic).
- Foreperiod. A uniform variable foreperiod makes RT fall as the foreperiod lengthens, typically as an exponential decay (Los, Kruijne and Meeter 2014, section on the family of foreperiod phenomena, Figure 1A [FT]). Buzz Hunt's foreperiod is uniform after the gate, and the dot brightening at trial start is its warning signal (code).
- There is no anticipation floor: a correct press 20 ms after the command, before the motor moves, scores as a hit (code).

Bearing: expect logged medians of roughly 300 to 500 ms; state them as command-to-press on this rig; flag presses faster than the motor can have been felt; and do not compare with published tactile RTs without the 90 to 100 ms rig allowance and the compatible response.

### Q5. Is the 150 ms buzz well above threshold on every finger?

- The part the code quotes. Precision Microdrives 310-103 [FT, product page]: rated 3 V (start 1.2 V, maximum 3.6 V), 12,200 rpm (203 Hz, arithmetic), 1.3 G normalised amplitude on a 100 g test load, lag 40 ms, rise 87 ms, stop 115 ms, active brake 45 ms, 10 mm diameter.
- Definitions. Lag is the time to 0.08 G; rise is from the start to 50 percent of the typical amplitude; stop is the time to stop from running; a higher drive voltage shortens lag and rise and lengthens stop; an ERM's frequency and amplitude cannot be set separately; Pacinian perception peaks between 200 and 300 Hz (Precision Microdrives AB-029 [FT]).
- The rig. Duty 78 percent on three fingers and 94 percent on the little finger (code, arithmetic). The measured 71 to 80 ms includes 1 to 6 ms of USB and firmware delay (`default.yaml` 1755-1756) and uses an acoustic criterion (5 SD above averaged room noise) that need not match the datasheet's 0.08 G; even so it is far beyond the rated 40 ms, which fits a lower effective voltage or a different part (AB-029 [FT]); the part number is not recorded. Half strength comes 100 to 140 ms after the command (design notes in `ranked.md`), so a 150 ms drive ends near half amplitude and the motor then coasts to a stop (about 115 ms at rated voltage) (arithmetic). The buzz's real frequency is unmeasured; under-driving would put it below the 203 Hz rating (AB-029 [FT]).
- How short a buzz can be. The shortest fingertip stimulus felt as vibration rather than a pulse had median durations of 28, 27, 25, 30, 28 and 28 ms at 40, 80, 150, 250, 300 and 590 Hz (35 adults, voice coil, 15 dB above threshold; Remache-Vinueza et al. 2025, Table 2 [FT]). Phone-motor control signals of 50 to 200 ms were rated best and longer ones irritating (Kaaresoja and Linjama 2005 [ABS]).
- Thresholds. Fingertip vibrotactile thresholds in 148 men: 109.4 dB (SD 8.3) at 125 Hz and 113.9 dB (SD 9.6) at 250 Hz re 10^-6 m/s^2 (Lundstrom et al. 2018, Table 1 [FT]), about 0.30 and 0.50 m/s^2 (arithmetic). Pacinian afferent thresholds are about 100 nm at 250 Hz (Manfredi et al. 2012 [FT]). If the fingertip received the datasheet's 1.3 G (12.7 m/s^2), the buzz would sit 28 to 33 dB above threshold (arithmetic, order of magnitude only: the page does not say whether its G is a peak or a peak-to-peak value, and the thresholds are for a probe); the coupling through the column and pad, and the drive level, are unmeasured.
- Pilot: every buzz in the two healthy free-play blocks was found (pilot).

Bearing: the pulse is very likely well above threshold, but not "at full amplitude", not equal across fingers (the little finger is driven harder and pitched differently), and its frequency is unknown. None of this threatens a ceiling accuracy; all of it belongs in the methods and claim limits.

### Q6. Do the other fingers feel the buzz, and can the participant hear it?

- In the skin. A fingertip vibration was measurable 64 mm away; decay was slowest at 200 to 250 Hz; the amplitude ratio was 25 to 53 percent at 16 mm and 0.4 to 1.2 percent at 64 mm; a 10 micrometre 250 Hz stimulus would drive a Pacinian afferent 6 cm away, and Pacinian receptive fields span one or more digits or the palm (Manfredi et al. 2012, Results "Measurement of skin vibrations" [FT]). Contact vibration spreads through the whole hand and stays above threshold across most of it near 250 Hz (Shao, Hayward and Visell 2016, Introduction and Results [FT]).
- Between fingers. A 100 Hz masker raised the index finger's detection threshold by 6.65 dB on the middle finger, 5.66 on the ring and 4.87 on the little finger; for a little-finger target, 3.59 (ring), 2.75 (middle) and 2.70 dB (index); no such gradient between hands (Tame, Moles and Holmes 2014, Experiments 1 to 4 [FT]).
- Through the device: four columns on one PLA base (hardware README). No crosstalk measurement exists (the modes-review lists an accelerometer bench as an after-collection item).
- Sound. The motors are loud enough to be timed by the laptop microphone (code). Hearing the vibration shortened finger RTs (Bao et al. 2019 [FT]); auditory-somatosensory pairs were detected faster than either alone, even out of spatial register (Murray et al. 2005 [ABS]). Where sources disagree: Holmes et al. 2020 [FT, Experiments 1 to 4 and meta-analysis] found no consistent RT benefit of sounds near rather than far from the hand in four experiments; the published effect pooled to 25.7 ms (18.5 to 32.9) with an asymmetric funnel plot suggesting publication bias.
- Masking practice: white noise over headphones to hide the actuators (Abrahamse et al. 2009 [FT]; Yeganeh et al. 2026 [FT]); earplugs or earmuffs except in the condition that tested the sound (Bao et al. 2019 [FT]).
- The little finger's higher duty spins its motor faster, so it sounds different (code; AB-029 [FT]).

Bearing: a neighbour error can come from spread through skin and chassis without any misreferral; the sound gives the buzz's onset away (and the silence of a catch trial) and may shorten RT by a few milliseconds. Neither changes a ceiling accuracy; both qualify B2, B3 and RT.

### Q7. What does the response-window ladder do to accuracy and RT, and is the level a measure?

- Deadlines. Deadlines that are too long have little to no effect; presenting deadline conditions in the same order to everyone confounds practice with the speed-accuracy manipulation; missed deadlines are normally signalled so the participant can adjust (Heitz 2014, section "SAT methodology", Deadlines and Deadline tracking [FT]). Buzz Hunt's shortest window, 1.5 s in the battery, is about three to four and a half times the pilot medians (pilot), and nothing tells the participant the window exists (code).
- The ladder as a counter. A perfect responder plays trials 1 to 6 at 3.0 s, 7 to 12 at 2.0 s and 13 to 16 at 1.5 s; 1.2 s needs 18 correct real trials and cannot be reached in 16 (code, arithmetic). Over 20,000 simulated blocks, the top level played was 2 in 85, 96, 99.4 and 100 percent of blocks at accuracies of 0.90, 0.95, 0.98 and 1.00, and at least 1 in 96.8 percent even at 0.80 (simulation). The pilot traces match (pilot).
- Catch windows ride on the ladder: for a 97 percent responder, 37 percent of catch trials run at 3.0 s, 36 percent at 2.0 s and 27 percent at 1.5 s (simulation), so false-alarm exposure varies by a factor of two.
- W5. Because the level is almost a function of trial number, centring RT within level removes most of any practice trend. With a true linear speed-up of 4 ms per trial, the last-third minus first-third contrast averaged -43.3 ms on the raw series and -4.7 ms on the centred one; W5 as registered passed 3, 8 and 12 percent of simulated cohorts at 2, 4 and 6 ms per trial, against 25, 73 and 96 percent for the raw series. A pure window effect of 30 ms per level and no practice passed the raw series 82 percent of the time and the centred one 2 percent (simulation, n = 10).
- Carry-over: in the 60 minute sitting, pass 2 starts at pass 1's final level (code), so its first trials run at a shorter window than pass 1's.

Bearing: for a healthy hand the ladder measures nothing but trial count; B5 cannot fail; W5 cannot tell practice from window; and the other battery modes freeze their windows for exactly this reason (`default.yaml` 2008-2013 comment: "nothing here moves a threshold, a window or a scoring rule").

### Q8. What can two catch trials say, and is d prime meaningful here?

- Corrections for extreme rates: Macmillan and Kaplan's replacement of 0 by 0.5/n and 1 by (n - 0.5)/n, or the log-linear method, adding 0.5 to the counts and 1 to the trial totals (Robson et al. 2024, section on d prime [FT]; Hautus 1995 [META]; Stanislaw and Todorov 1999 [ABS]). Robson et al. also recommend equal numbers of signal and noise trials and as many trials as practical, and show that near ceiling groups become indistinguishable [FT].
- Here the "hit" is any press on a real trial, near 1 for everyone. With all 16 real trials answered, a person's d prime is 2.86, 1.89 or 0.92 for 0, 1 or 2 false alarms; with 15 answered, 2.32, 1.35 or 0.38 (arithmetic). It is the false-alarm count under another name.
- B3 as coded passes in full only when nobody in the cohort has a false alarm (the interval of ten zeros has zero width): with a true per-trial false-alarm probability of 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 or 0.10, B3 passes in full in 82, 67, 36 and 12 percent of simulated cohorts; the rest print "direction only" or fail (simulation). Exact one-sided 95 percent upper bounds for no false alarm: 13.9 percent in 20 catch trials, 9.8 percent in 29, 9.5 percent in 30 (arithmetic).
- Early presses (presses during the foreperiod, which restart the wait) are already counted per stage (code). They sample guessing on every trial rather than on two.
- No source was found that reports catch-trial false alarms for suprathreshold finger localisation; near-threshold detection studies are not comparable. Pilot: 0 of 2 (pilot).

Bearing: B3 can say "no false alarm seen in 20 chances", not "under 10 percent"; per-person d prime adds nothing to the count; the early-press rate is the better-powered guessing index.

### Q9. How large is a healthy tactile span on the fingers, how is it tested, and what can four trials estimate?

- The finger task closest to Buzz Hunt: the Tactual Span. Four fingers of each hand rest on keyboard keys, eyes covered; the examiner touches the knuckles for 1 s each; the participant presses the touched fingers' keys in order; three trials per length from 2, the length grows when at least one of the three is right, up to 9; the score is the longest length recalled. Thirty adults aged 20 to 29: forward 5.30 (SD 1.26), backward 4.93 (SD 1.01); digit span forward 6.13 (0.93) and visuospatial 5.97 (0.81); no significant difference between modalities in this age group (Levi and Heled 2024, Methods 2.2.3, Table 2, Results [FT]).
- Where sources disagree: in 36 adults aged 25 to 35, forward Tactual Span was worse than digit and visuospatial span in every age group (Heled and Levi 2024, Results [FT], values in figures only). The task has adequate internal consistency and construct validity in 140 young adults (Heled et al. 2021 [ABS]); a one-hand version was valid with no difference between hands in 145 people (Heled 2025 [ABS]); left-handers scored lower on backward recall only (Heled et al. 2026 [ABS]).
- The source B4 cites. Yeganeh et al. 2026 [FT, Methods 2.1 to 2.4, Results 3.1 and 3.2]: ten adults aged 26 to 40, nine voice-coil actuators in a 3 x 3 grid on the forearm, 100 Hz, 500 ms pulses, 450 ms apart, each site at most once per sequence, white noise over headphones, training with feedback and none in test, a 3-up 1-down staircase (about 79 percent correct) to six reversals. Ordered recall threshold 3.84 items (SD 0.3), unordered 4.88 (SD 0.8); ordered accuracy about 79 percent at 4 items, 51 at 5 and 9 to 0 at 6 or more.
- Serial recall of finger taps (index, middle, ring of both hands, about one per second, six items, recall by lifting the fingers): 0.58 (SD 0.21) of items correct in quiet; two practice trials per block, used because unfamiliarity lowers tactile memory scores (Roe et al. 2024, Experiment 1 Method, Experiment 2 Results [FT]). Recognition of finger-touch sequences suffered under articulatory suppression, so people recode touches verbally and spatially (Mahrer and Miles 2002 [ABS]). Tactile working memory was more limited and more variable than visual in 39 young adults (Bliss and Hamalainen 2005 [ABS]). With simultaneous fingertip stimuli people reported up to three by counting but up to six by partial report (Auvray, Gallace and Spence 2011 [ABS]); the partial-report method for brief tactile displays on the fingers goes back to Bliss et al. 1966 [META], whose figures were not read.
- The rig differs from every reference task: four fingers of one hand, so every sequence past four items repeats a finger; 150 ms buzzes at 2.5 per second against about 1 per second elsewhere; a 1-up 1-down ladder from 2 with one trial per length; four trials; no practice (code). No study was found that varied presentation rate for finger vibration sequences.
- What four trials give (simulation, a person's accuracy falling from 95 percent through 50 percent at s50 items with a logistic slope of 0.6 items; Yeganeh's ordered curve is matched at s50 of about 5). At s50 = 4.5: `max_correct` is 3 in 39 percent of blocks, 4 in 42, 5 in 16. At 5.0: mean 4.0, 33 percent at the cap of 5. B4's band of 3 to 5 holds 93 to 99 percent of blocks for s50 of 4.0 to 6.0. The correlation between `max_correct` and a person's true s50 is 0.43 to 0.67; the test-retest ICC(2,1) of `max_correct` is 0.17, 0.31 and 0.50 for true between-person SDs of 0.5, 0.8 and 1.2 items. Starting at 3 (cap 6) raises the ICC from 0.28 to 0.37 and cuts the share at the cap from 29 to 9 percent; six trials from 2 give 0.44 (true s50 4.8, SD 0.8). A logistic fit pooled over a cohort of ten recovers the group s50 within about 0.7 items (90 percent range).
- The Hebb trial is the third span trial; in a single block it is a first exposure, so it is a novel trial to the participant, as the design already notes (design doc 981-986). In a 60 minute sitting the same participant meets the same hidden sequence in pass 2 when the length matches (code).

Bearing: B4 is a feasibility check that a healthy cohort passes; `max_correct` from four trials is a coarse, capped and unreliable individual score; the rig's span is not comparable with the Tactual Span or with Yeganeh's thresholds; the cohort statement should rest on a pooled fit.

### Q10. What reliability should each output have?

- RT. Simple and two-choice vibrotactile RT medians retested at ICC 0.90 each over three sessions in about three weeks, with 20-trial short versions; within-person coefficients of variation 10.9 and 9.5 percent; detection thresholds retested at ICC 0.65 (Mikkelsen et al. 2020, Table 1 and Results [FT]).
- Simulation for a 16-trial median (between-person SD 50 to 80 ms, within-person SD 60 to 100 ms, day-to-day SD 0 to 20 ms): test-retest ICC(2,1) medians 0.74 to 0.94 (5th percentiles 0.35 to 0.82 at n = 10); within-session split-half, Spearman-Brown corrected, 0.86 to 0.94 (simulation).
- Accuracy. A locognosia test retested at ICC 0.924 (median nerve) and 0.859 (ulnar) in 39 patients (Jerosch-Herold, Rosen and Shepstone 2006 [ABS]); that range comes from injured hands. Healthy controls made zero to two misreferrals in 90 (Weber et al. 2023 [FT]), which leaves no between-person variance to retest.
- Span: poor to moderate from four trials (Q9, simulation). No test-retest study of the Tactual Span was found; its reports give internal consistency only [ABS]. Trials needed: the Spearman-Brown formula applied to the simulated 0.28 at four trials puts an ICC of 0.7 at about 24 trials (arithmetic, a rough guide because ladder trials are not parallel items); Yeganeh's staircases ran to six reversals in sessions of about 40 minutes [FT].

Bearing: RT is the output worth a reliability claim ("good" is realistic); accuracy is not estimable; span is poor to moderate, not moderate.

### Q11. Learning within the block, and handedness

- Practice. Two-choice vibrotactile RT changed little across sessions: 434.8, 430.9 and 417.1 ms (Mikkelsen et al. 2020, Table 1 [FT]). In the pilot, one code's median fell from 376 ms at level 0 to 322 ms at level 1 (pilot), which the ladder makes impossible to attribute (Q7). Twenty hours of training shifted near-threshold mislocalisation patterns (Schweizer et al. 2001 [ABS]).
- Handedness. No difference between dominant and non-dominant hands in controls' localisation error (t(32) = 1.1, p = 0.28; Weber et al. 2023 [FT]); no difference between homologous fingers in spatial acuity, which declined from index to middle to ring (Vega-Bermudez and Johnson 2001 [ABS]); no hand effect on the one-hand Tactual Span (Heled 2025 [ABS]); dominant fingers not more independent (Hager-Ross and Schieber 2000 [ABS]). Where sources disagree: near threshold, the left hand localised more accurately than the right in eight people (Schweizer et al. 2000 [ABS]); left-handers scored lower on backward Tactual Span (Heled et al. 2026 [ABS]).
- Per finger, near threshold: the ring finger was most accurate in one study (Schweizer et al. 2000 [ABS]); accuracy fell from index to ring and was lowest on the little finger in another (Manser-Smith et al. 2018 [FT]).

Bearing: left-handers play their non-dominant hand here, with small expected effects; report them as a sensitivity split. Per-finger differences on this rig also carry the little finger's stronger drive and the motors' different delays.

### Q12. Scoring, feedback and instructions

- Instructions, payoffs with criterion times and deadlines each shift the speed-accuracy balance (Heitz 2014, "SAT manipulations" [FT]).
- Code: the stage card asks for the right finger and says nothing about speed; the score pays 3 points under 500 ms and 1 point over it, with a streak bonus, on a visible SCORE; the green dot marks correct answers and survived catch trials; the timeout shows nothing (Section 1.3). The run sheet has no Buzz Hunt line.

Bearing: the block runs a speed payoff that participants must infer. Since median RT is the continuous primary number, the instruction should say what matters, and the thesis should name the payoff.

### Q13. What can a healthy study claim clinically?

- Stroke: a scoping review of somatosensory deficits after stroke gives no single prevalence figure in its abstract (Kessner, Bingel and Thomalla 2016 [META]). Somatosensory loss ranges widely by modality and body area: 7 to 53 percent impaired tactile sensation, 31 to 89 percent stereognosis, 34 to 64 percent proprioception in 70 first strokes (Connell, Lincoln and Radford 2008 [ABS]). SENSe: 50 chronic stroke survivors, 10 hours of discrimination training against exposure, between-group change 11.1 SSD points (CI 3.0 to 19.2), held at 6 weeks and 6 months (Carey, Macdonell and Matyas 2011 [ABS]).
- Carpal tunnel: 104 patients randomised after decompression; locognosia (0 to 56), control against sensory relearning, 44.8 against 44.9 at 6 weeks (difference -0.09, CI -3.28 to 3.09) and 46.4 against 46.5 at 12 weeks; only self-reported hand function differed (Jerosch-Herold et al. 2016, Tables 2 and 3 [FT]).
- Nerve repair: the discriminating measure was the error of localisation in millimetres within the injured territory (t(17) = 4.5, p < 0.001); misreferrals were rare in both groups and did not differ between patients and controls at group level, although three patients stood out individually (Weber et al. 2023, Results [FT]). Home re-education improved locognosia at long term in a sequentially allocated cohort of 52 (Antonopoulos et al. 2019 [ABS]).
- Focal hand dystonia: eight weeks of braille training improved spatial acuity in patients and controls and the Fahn scale in 10 patients (Zeuner et al. 2002 [ABS]).

Bearing: the healthy study can report feasibility, ceiling localisation, RT and a coarse span on this rig; it cannot claim therapy, misreferral mapping, finger gnosis or clinical sensitivity. The confusion matrix mirrors the part of Weber's tool that did not separate groups.

How to use them: report the rig's numbers as its own, measured command to press with an unmasked coin motor under the answering finger, a uniform foreperiod and about 90 to 100 ms of rig delay. The published values frame the order of magnitude; none is a norm to test against.

### 6.2 Reliability and practice expectations

- Median localisation RT: "good" is realistic. Vibrotactile simple and two-choice RT retested at ICC 0.90 over about three weeks (Mikkelsen et al. 2020 [FT]); simulated 16-trial medians give 0.74 to 0.94 between sittings and 0.86 to 0.94 split-half within a block (simulation). The within-session split-half says nothing about day-to-day stability.
- Accuracy: not estimable in healthy hands; the locognosia test's ICC of 0.86 to 0.92 comes from injured hands (Jerosch-Herold et al. 2006 [ABS]).
- Span `max_correct` from four trials: poor to moderate (ICC 0.17 to 0.50, simulation); no published retest of a finger span was found.
- Practice: little change in vibrotactile two-choice RT across sessions (434.8, 430.9, 417.1 ms; Mikkelsen et al. 2020 [FT]); within the block, practice and window are inseparable while the ladder moves (simulation).

### 6.3 Claims to avoid

- A localisation threshold, a tactile acuity or a detection threshold from this block: the pulse is fixed and far above threshold.
- Somatotopic misreferral, or "errors cluster on neighbours", from B2 at this n.
- Finger gnosis or finger agnosia: pressing the vibrated finger is the direct sensorimotor route (Leonard via Proctor and Schneider 2018 [FT]; Anema et al. 2008 [ABS]).
- A tactile working memory capacity, a Tactual Span or a match to Yeganeh's thresholds from four trials capped at 5.
- Hebb learning from the battery block.
- A per-person d prime, or "under 10 percent false alarms" from 20 catch trials.
- The window level as skill, difficulty reached or improvement.
- "Practice" or "no practice" from W5 while the ladder moves.
- That the buzz reaches full amplitude, runs at 200 Hz, sits in the Pacinian optimum or is equal on every finger.
- Per-finger differences as finger differences: the little finger's motor is driven harder and the motors' delays differ by up to 9 ms.
- Absolute RT comparisons with published tactile RTs without the rig allowance and the compatible response.
- "Tactile span caps far below visual span".
- Any treatment effect for carpal tunnel or nerve repair (Jerosch-Herold et al. 2016 [FT]), or a stroke prevalence figure without its modality and body area.

### Simulation assumptions (for Sections 2 and 4)

Window ladder: a scratch copy of `WindowLadder` (promote on 6 correct of the last 8 with at least 6 recorded, demote on 2 misses in the last 4, history cleared on a move, catch trials outside it), 16 real trials with independent correct answers at a fixed accuracy, 20,000 blocks per accuracy. Catch windows: 2 catch slots uniform among 18 positions, 97 percent accuracy. W5: 10 people, 16 real trials at 97 percent accuracy, RT = person mean (330 ms plus a between-person normal with SD 60 ms) + slope x trial index + window effect x level + ex-Gaussian noise with total SD 70 ms (tau 56 ms), correct trials only, per-person median of the last third minus the first third (a third = round(n/3), at least 2), raw or centred on each level's median, pass when the mean difference's 95 percent t interval (t(9) = 2.262) sits below zero, 1,500 cohorts per cell. B2: wrong-finger errors Binomial(160, rate); each error follows a middle or ring target with probability 0.74 (Weber et al. 2023's healthy share); under "random" a middle or ring error is adjacent with probability 2/3 and an index or little error with 1/3; under "adjacent" each error is adjacent with the stated share; registered test = exact one-sided binomial at 0.5 with the share above 0.5; conditional test = Monte Carlo Poisson-binomial tail (4,000 draws); 6,000 cohorts per cell. B3: 10 people x 2 catch trials, false alarm Bernoulli(rate), the notebook's t-interval rule, 50,000 cohorts. Span: a person's probability of a correct replay at length L is 0.95 / (1 + exp((L - s50) / 0.6)); 1-up 1-down from 2 (or 3), floor 2; test-retest by two independent administrations in 30 people with s50 normal around 4.5 or 4.8 and SD 0.5 to 1.2; ICC(2,1) by the McGraw and Wong absolute-agreement single-measure formula; pooled fit by maximum likelihood with the slope fixed; 1,500 to 20,000 runs per cell. RT reliability: 10 people, 16 trials a sitting, between-person SD 50 or 80 ms, within-person SD 60 to 100 ms (ex-Gaussian), day-to-day SD 0 or 20 ms, medians, ICC(2,1) between two sittings, odd-even split-half with Spearman-Brown, 1,500 runs per cell. Error counts at 1 to 3 percent: Binomial(160, rate), 100,000 draws.
