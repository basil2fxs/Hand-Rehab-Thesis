HEALTHY BASELINE STUDY
Design for the psychology-student cohort, Thesis Project A

Status: 2026-09-24. ONE SITTING per participant on ONE BOARD, the
right-hand device, in TWO PASSES. Pass 1 plays the nine one-hand
modes once; after a 3 minute rest, pass 2 plays Reaction, Force Pilot
and Chords again with the same counts. Twelve blocks, about 44
minutes on the rig and about 60 minutes booked per person, run as one
collection day at the end of semester. No return visit. The battery
preset that runs it is protocol.presets.study_battery, id
healthy_one_hand_v2.

The authority on the design is Basil's own plan file, "Data
Collection Plan.md", under
Uni/Semester 2/Thesis/Current Thesis Part B/00_Key Facts/. This
document is the working detail behind that plan and follows it.

HOW THE DESIGN GOT HERE (every change made before any participant):

  - Two visits a week apart, then a pre/mid/post sitting: both gone.
  - 4 September 2026: ONE PASS, both hands, eleven blocks
    (healthy_one_pass_v1). It dropped R3 and P2, which needed a
    block played twice, and had no test-retest of any kind.
  - 3 September 2026: echo moved to the Simon rule, which dropped E2
    (the Hebb repeat only exists under the ladder rule).
  - 23 September 2026: A5, A6, S6 and S7 added so every mode has a
    pre-specified row; the study's rhythm buzz put on the beat;
    Syllables made a choice task with a late prompt; E3 set against
    chance.
  - 24 September 2026: ONE BOARD. Only one hand device will be used
    this semester, and the printed chassis is the right hand (the
    mirrored left half is CAD only). So every block is the right
    hand, for every participant; Mirror, which needs a board on each
    hand, is not played; and the minutes the second board's blocks
    used to take now buy a second pass of the three core modes,
    which gives the design the within-session test-retest it did not
    have. The Syllables prompt became progressive (Section 1.4).

What one board costs, and the thesis says it in the same breath as
the results:

  - No within-person dominant against non-dominant contrast. R2, C5,
    Rh3 and M2 are dropped, with the paired chapter that carried
    them (Section 4.4 now compares right- and left-handers between
    people, exploratory).
  - No Mirror: M1, M2 and W2 are dropped. No cross-hand chords: C4 is
    dropped.
  - Echo on four lanes: a sequence past four items holds every lane,
    so a wrong finger is a sequence lane by construction and E3 is
    dropped for the one-board rig; E1 reads the four-lane Simon band
    (Section 1.11).
  - Left-handers play their NON-dominant hand. Anyone can take part.
    Everyone is in every pooled number (the device, the task and the
    hand are the same for all); Section 4.4 sets the left-handers
    against the right-handers, and Section 4.9 reruns every check on
    the right-handers alone as a sensitivity analysis.

What the second pass buys:

  - Within-session test-retest for Reaction, Force Pilot and Chords:
    ICC(2,1) with its exact interval, SEM, MDC95, Bland-Altman and
    the pass 2 minus pass 1 shift, read against the reliability class
    Section 1 predicted (T1 to T5, Section 4.5). About twenty minutes
    apart in one seat with one calibration, so the ICC is an upper
    bound on day-to-day reliability and the MDC95 a lower bound on a
    real change between days. Between-day test-retest is still the
    next student's first job.
  - R3 comes back: no practice effect on median RT from pass 1 to
    pass 2, as an equivalence test with a 20 ms margin.

Two contrasts are named in Section 1 as not available rather than as
dropped checks, because the design never claimed them: the
choice-minus-simple reaction contrast (only the choice sub-mode is
played) and the RAS-against-fixed-cadence contrast (the battery
carries no fixed-cadence control block).

Sample size: Section 2.1. Bonett (2002) is back in view for the
reliability rows, and at n = 8 to 12 it says plainly that an ICC
interval will span two or three bands, which is why T1 to T5 are
reported against their prediction rather than tested.

Section 1, what each mode measures and the literature behind it,
still carries the study; where a mode's headline assumed both hands,
the one-board note in that mode's section says what changes. Sections
2 to 5 are written for the two-pass one-board sitting.

No participant has been run: every number about people below is a
prediction or a literature figure, and every duration is either
measured headless (marked M) or arithmetic from the shipped config
(marked A). Every literature number is cited; numbers that could not
be verified are marked as such and are not load-bearing. Lighthouse
was retired from the battery on 2026-09-03 (Section 1.9).

Why this study exists. No patients are available this semester. The
thesis still needs a results chapter with real hands on the device,
so the rig is turned into a measurement instrument and characterised
on healthy adults: what does each game measure in a normal hand, how
wide is that measurement across people, how repeatable is it inside
a sitting, and does each game reproduce the effect the literature
says a healthy hand shows. That is a normative, reliability and
construct study, not a treatment study, and it is a legitimate thesis
result on its own. A device whose numbers sit in a sensible range,
repeat, and reproduce known effects is the precondition for any
later patient work, and a device whose numbers do not tells the next
student what to fix first.

What this study cannot show, and the thesis must say so in the same
breath. A healthy cohort says nothing about sensitivity to
impairment (whether a stroke or carpal tunnel hand scores
differently), nothing about responsiveness (whether the numbers move
with recovery), nothing about the 65 to 80 percent challenge band or
any other therapy claim, and nothing about clinical validity against
an accepted assessment. The sitting adds three limits on top of that.
First, the only repeatability is within-session and for three modes,
so it bounds day-to-day reliability from above and says nothing for
the other six modes. Second, the normative table describes the
right-handed students who sat on the collection day, on their
dominant hand, not a population, and is read against literature
values as an order-of-magnitude plausibility check only. Third, the
within-block improvement in Section 4.5 is a change in PERFORMANCE
inside a few minutes of practice, not learning: conditions that raise
end-of-practice scores can leave a delayed retention test untouched
or worse, and the delayed test is the better indicator of learning
(Kantak and Winstein 2012, Behavioural Brain Research). The
known-effect checks in Section 4.6 are construct checks: the game
reproduces an effect the literature predicts, which is evidence the
measurement is of the right thing, not evidence the device is a
valid clinical instrument.


DECISIONS IN ONE SCREEN
=======================

- One sitting, ONE board, the right-hand device, for everybody.
  Booking 60 minutes: 15 minutes of consent and intake off the clock,
  then a PLAY ALL run measured (M) at medians of 43.7 to 44.2 minutes
  over 48 sittings on six codes, range 43.2 to 44.9, none over the
  budget of 45 or the hard stop at 50 (Section 2.3).
- Twelve blocks. Pass 1: reaction, rhythm, echo, force pilot,
  chords, buzz hunt, pattern, adaptive, syllables, each once. Pass 2,
  after the rest: reaction, force pilot, chords, in the order pass 1
  played them. Mirror is not played.
- One 3 minute rest, between the passes: it is the test-retest
  interval. It counts down on the NEXT UP card and holds the Start
  button for the first 60 seconds; the rest actually taken is logged.
  One 60 second stretch note in pass 1, never holding the button.
- Two mode orders (A timing work first, B force and vibration work
  first), assigned by participant code. With one hand there is no
  hand order to cross.
- Anyone can take part, right- or left-handed. Everyone plays the
  same right-hand device, the dominant hand of a right-hander and the
  non-dominant hand of a left-hander, and everyone is in the pooled
  sample. Handedness is analysed as a between-person factor (Section
  4.4) and a sensitivity rerun drops the left-handers (Section 4.9).
- Every ladder that can be frozen without changing the task is
  frozen by config: the reaction response window and the chords sync
  window. Four modes keep a ladder that IS the task, so for them the
  trial index inside a block means difficulty and not time (Section
  4.5b).
- Participants are P01 upward. The code goes in the login NAME field
  and keys the sessions tree, the index, the notebook and the hidden
  sequences. Names live only in the consent log.
- What the participant sees: after each block, that block's own
  first trials against its own last trials, in the mode's own words,
  and at PLAY ALL DONE a TODAY table with one row per mode. Nothing
  on it calls a change real: the only noise floor is within-session
  (Section 4.5a).
- Primary outputs: the normative table from pass 1 (n, median, IQR,
  range and every participant's own point), the known-effect checks
  (Table 1) and within-block checks (Table 2) on pass 1, the
  within-session reliability table T1 to T5 (pass 1 against pass 2),
  internal consistency inside a block, the feasibility numbers, the
  handedness comparison and the right-handers-only sensitivity rerun.
  One tidy CSV.
- Reaction is the stability anchor. It is the one mode expected NOT
  to improve inside its own block, so a trend there reads as warm-up
  or drift and it calibrates how much of every other mode's
  within-block trend is the same thing; it is also the reliability
  anchor, with a PVT literature to hold its ICC against.


1. WHAT A HEALTHY COHORT CAN PROVE, PER MODE
============================================

Format per mode: primary metric(s) and where they come from; the
known healthy-adult effect the game must reproduce; expected
test-retest reliability class; honest claim limits. "block_stats"
means the mode's own block_stats() dict, folded into
metadata.json under block_summary.<mode>; "notebook" means the
offline re-score in analysis/session_analysis.ipynb.

Reliability classes use Koo and Li (2016): below 0.5 poor, 0.5 to
0.75 moderate, 0.75 to 0.9 good, above 0.9 excellent, and are
predictions to be tested, not promises. This design does not test
them: nothing in it is measured twice (Section 4.5). Every class
below is left standing because it says which metrics the
test-retest study that follows should start with.

1.1 Reaction (choice, one hand per block)
-----------------------------------------
Primary: median_rt_ms, lapse_like_rate, n_false_start_total /
n_attempts, accuracy (block_stats.reaction). Secondary: sd_rt_ms,
p10_rt_ms, slope_rt_ms_per_trial, spearman_rho_rt_vs_fp.
Known effects to reproduce:
  R1 The exponential foreperiod works: cohort median of the
     Spearman rho between RT and foreperiod near zero (|rho| under
     0.2) and false starts under 10 percent of attempts. This is the
     anticipation control the mode was built for (Niemi and Naatanen
     1981; Naatanen 1971, both cited in reaction.py).
  R2 Dominant hand faster than non-dominant. The dominant advantage
     is well replicated for tapping speed and consistency (Peters
     1980; Hubel et al. 2013) and is small for reaction time.
     DROPPED on the one-board rig (24 September 2026): every block
     is the right hand, so nobody plays both, and right- against
     left-handers on one hand would compare people, not hands.
  R3 No practice effect on median RT from pass 1 to pass 2: the 95
     percent CI of pass 2 minus pass 1 inside +/- 20 ms, by two
     one-sided tests (equivalence, the form F3 uses). 20 ms is a
     little over one 60 Hz frame (16.7 ms), the rig's own timing
     grain, and about 5 percent of a four-choice RT. The basis: the
     PVT family the block is modelled on shows no change in median
     RT or lapses over 16 repeated administrations (Basner et al.
     2017, Sleep). Restored 24 September 2026, when the second pass
     made it computable; it had been dropped under the one-pass
     design.
Reliability class: good to excellent for median RT (the PVT
tradition; the block is 20 scorable trials, near the validated 3 min
PVT-B of Basner, Mollicone and Dinges 2011). Tested as T1 (Section
4.5): the prediction the within-session ICC is read against.
Claim limits: absolute RTs are device-specific (60 Hz display, force
threshold clock, unmeasured panel latency; Woods 2015 and Plant and
Turner 2009 in reaction.py) and measured to an audio-tactile-visual
cue under the shipped cue defaults. Report them as this device's
numbers, never against published button-press norms. Simple RT is
not run (time), so the choice-versus-simple contrast is not
available.

1.2 Pattern, SRTT (dominant hand only, short form)
--------------------------------------------------
Primary: session_learning_score_ms (probe minus flanking trained
takes, block_stats.pattern.probe_scores) and accuracy_rebound_pct.
Secondary: per-take mean RT curve, random-take baseline RT.
Known effects to reproduce:
  P1 Positive learning score in the block (cohort mean above
     zero).
     The probe rebound is one of the most replicated effects in
     cognitive psychology (Nissen and Bullemer 1987: 327 to 163 ms;
     Kal et al. 2016 meta-analysis: 69 ms mean rebound in healthy
     and less-affected hands, cited in pattern.py). With the short
     form's smaller exposure expect tens of ms, not 69.
  P2 DROPPED. It needed the pattern block played a second time, a
     larger learning score on the second go and a trained-take RT
     below the first block's. One pass means one pattern block, so
     this study has no retention probe of any kind. Even when it
     was in, the claim was modest: end-of-practice performance and
     learning that survives a night are different things (Kantak
     and Winstein 2012).
  P3 Accuracy drops on probe takes (rebound above zero).
Reliability class: POOR for the individual learning score, and this
must be said in the thesis before the data comes in. The 2023
meta-analysis of SRTT test-retest correlations pooled r = 0.28 (95
percent CI 0.16 to 0.40; Oliveira, Hayiou-Thomas and Henderson
2023, Royal Society Open Science), with no moderator rescuing it.
The group-level effect is reliable; the individual score is not a
trait. Neither number is computed here (Section 4.5); the
prediction stands for whoever runs the test-retest study, and it
says to expect a low ICC and to give the learning score no MDC.
Claim limits: dominant hand only, so no asymmetry. Single-session
rebounds can be temporary adaptation (Trofimova 2020 in
pattern.py); P2 is the memory claim. Never tell participants a
sequence exists (Boyd and Winstein, in pattern.py).
Riff files: a sequence file loaded on the study laptop
(config/pattern_sequence.yaml, Settings > Riff file) REPLACES the
built-in ten-take layout and the name-seeded riff for every pattern
block. Everything above assumes the built-in material, so either run
the cohort with no file loaded, or load the SAME file for every
participant and leave it alone for the whole study. The file's
schedule id (the sha256 of its bytes) lands in every block's
metadata, so a mid-study swap is recoverable in the analysis, but it
splits the cohort into two groups that cannot be pooled. A file with
explicit: true tells the participant the riff, which is a different
design from this one and is excluded from the implicit measure.

1.3 Chords (one hand, short form, both passes)
---------------------------------------------
One-board note (24 September 2026): the block plays the right hand's
four fingers, 40 trials (two sub-blocks of 20), in both passes. No
cross-hand chords exist on one board, so C4 and C5 are dropped and
the cross-hand metrics below are not produced.
Size mix (chords.size_mix, 24 September 2026, before any
participant): each sub-block of 20 deals 4 single fingers (one per
finger), 10 pairs, 5 triples and 1 four-finger chord. Eight trials in
ten use two or three fingers, where every chord has a quiet finger to
keep still; the quad has no quiet finger and runs every motor at once
(the firmware's source says four motors overload the shared driver),
so it is rare and its buzz is the arpeggio. Singles are the baseline
for C6 and for the one-finger enslaving matrix, and they are kept out
of every number that says chord (C1 to C3, T4, T5).
Primary: median_er (enslaving ratio, quiet-finger leak over target
press), median_span_ms (chord togetherness), clean hit rate
(chord_outcome_classes.hit / all), per-hand versions in per_hand;
cross-hand: hit_rate_mirror, hit_rate_nonmirror, median_lag_ms,
bilateral_deficit.
Known effects to reproduce:
  C1 Cohort median ER under 0.15. Healthy hands leak roughly 5 to 15
     percent of instructed force at light effort (Abolins et al.
     2020, cited in chords.py: 8 to 10 percent at about 25 percent
     MVC).
  C2 The computed difficulty rank D predicts outcome: negative
     Spearman correlation between D and per-chord hit rate, positive
     between D and per-chord median ER, on the cohort-pooled
     per-chord table. This tests the adjacency rule (enslaving
     largest between neighbours, Zatsiorsky, Li and Latash 2000).
  C3 Ring finger the most enslaved: in the cohort-mean
     chord-conditioned matrix the ring column carries the largest
     leak (Hager-Ross and Schieber 2000; Chiang 2004, in chords.py).
  C4 Cross-hand mirror chords hit more often and land tighter than
     non-mirror chords (the symmetry advantage: Kelso 1984; Haken,
     Kelso and Bunz 1985; Mechsner 2001, in chords.py).
  C5 Dominant versus non-dominant ER: no strong prediction. Force
     coordination studies in young adults often find no dominance
     difference (Scientific Reports 2025, "Comparable neural and
     behavioural performance in dominant and non-dominant hands
     during grasping tasks"), so report the paired result with its
     CI and treat a null as expected.
  (C4 and C5 are DROPPED on the one-board rig: see the note above.)
  C6 The chord cost: the first press comes later the more fingers
     the chord asks for. Per participant, the least-squares slope of
     median RT on chord size over one, two and three fingers (the
     quad left out: once per twenty, and a different buzz); the
     cohort median slope above zero, one-sample Wilcoxon, one-sided.
     Healthy students pressed one, two and three keys in 510, 632 and
     762 ms, about 126 ms per extra finger (Verwey 2023; the chord
     effect first shown by Seibel 1962). Added 24 September 2026
     with the singles, before any participant, and in the Holm
     family.
  Described beside them: the size table (RT, completion, span, ER
     and clean hits per size) and the one-finger enslaving matrix,
     the classical single-finger condition (Zatsiorsky, Li and Latash
     2000) that the chord-conditioned matrices upper-bound.
Reliability class: moderate for ER (a ratio of two noisy peaks
normalised by that day's calibration), good for span_ms. Tested as
T4 and T5 (Section 4.5).
Claim limits: ER and the matrices are device- and posture-specific,
and the chord matrices are chord-conditioned (an upper bound on the
single-finger cell); literature values are order-of-magnitude context
only. The short form gives 32 chords and 8 singles a block, so
per-chord and per-single cells are cohort-pooled, never per person.

1.4 Syllables (played once, two ceiling checks)
-----------------------------------------------
A paediatric reading game: a word is heard and seen, then its
syllables fall one set at a time as four written options over four
fingers, and the child presses the finger under the right chunk. If a
set is still unanswered late in its fall, the right finger, and only
that finger, buzzes once. The delay is progressive per word (0.6,
0.75, then 0.9 of the fall, then no prompt, one step later for each
answer found alone and one step back after an error; Walker 2008 for
progressive over constant delay) and never earlier than the reader's
own median answer time plus 300 ms (syllables.py PROMPT; the
research note syllables-dyslexia-rework.md, PROMPT TIMING). The
battery plays 12 words at the mid foil rung (rung 3).
25 September 2026: the mode gained age profiles for readers past 9
(syllables_profiles.py; the review is syllables-all-ages.md). The
battery pins the classic profile, so this block, S6 and S7 are as
pre-registered whatever a participant's age. One fix reaches it, and
it removes items rather than changing the design: a foil that sounds
like the target (ter against tir or tur, cap against kap, bas against
bass) is no longer drawn, since a set holding one has no answer by
ear. Speech on the study Mac stays the system voice; the recorded
voice, when it exists, replaces it and the methods should say which
the block ran with (the speech events in raw.csv name the file or
the voice).
Adults have no reading deficit to measure, so the block is not a
skill result. What it can check is the game itself, on fluent readers:
  S6 Fluent adults answer before the prompt: cohort median of the
     unprompted correct rate above 0.9. A lower number says the
     material, the foils or the timing are wrong for anybody, which
     is a bug report before it is a finding.
  S7 The late prompt is rarely needed: cohort median of the share of
     right answers that came after the prompt under 0.1. This says
     the prompt's delay leaves a fluent reader room to answer first,
     which is what makes the unprompted rate the learning measure for
     a child.
Both are decided on the interval (median and its order-statistic
interval against the reference), not on a test: at a ceiling every
value can be identical, which a test reads as "no variation" when it
is the expected answer. Syllables stays out of the reliability and
within-block tables.

1.5 Adaptive (played once, two direction checks)
------------------------------------------------
The controller ramps a healthy hand to bpm_max (180) and the config's
own headless measurement says the trailing hit rate only re-enters
the 65 to 80 band around trial 53, past the 40-trial block. A
healthy 40-trial block therefore measures the cap, not the band
keeping, and the band-keeping claim stays on the headless simulation
already in the config commentary. Adaptive is played once, late in
the sitting on the dominant hand. What a healthy block can check is
the direction of the closed loop on a real hand:
  A5 The pace rises for a healthy hand: cohort median of bpm_rise
     (the block's top pace minus its starting pace) above zero.
  A6 While it rises, the hand stays at or above the band floor:
     cohort median hit rate above 0.65. The controller's steps do not
     push a healthy hand into failure inside 40 trials.
Both are decided on the interval, like S6 and S7. bpm_max_reached,
bpm_final and the share of the block at the cap stay descriptive, and
adaptive stays out of the reliability and within-block tables.

1.6 Rhythm (one hand, one fixed track)
--------------------------------------
One-board note (24 September 2026): the block plays the right hand.
Rh1 is read on the right hand over everyone; Rh3 is DROPPED, since
nobody plays both hands.
Primary: signed mean asynchrony per hand (time_difference_ms on hit
rows, notebook sec_rhythm), asynchrony SD, mean absolute offset
(block_summary.beat_offset_stats.beat_offset_abs_mean_ms), hit rate;
interval CV and the tempo-tracking r from sec_tap_variability.
Known effects to reproduce:
  Rh1 Negative mean asynchrony: taps precede the beat by tens of ms
      (Repp 2005, Psychonomic Bulletin and Review; Repp and Su
      2013). One-sample test per hand, mean below zero.
  Rh2 Asynchrony SD in the tens of ms for a comfortable tempo (Rose
      et al. 2019 report about 39 ms finger-tapping asynchrony,
      cited in docs/research/new_modes/movement-disorders.md).
  Rh3 No strong dominance prediction; report paired.
Reliability class: good for asynchrony SD and absolute offset,
moderate for the signed mean (bias is small relative to noise).
Cue timing in the battery: the preset sets rhythm.tactile_mode to
on_beat, so the music, the falling note and the buzz all land on the
beat and every participant synchronises to the same pacing signal.
Free play leads the beat by 350 ms and adapts the lead to each
player, which helps a player who reacts to the buzz, but that makes
the block a reaction task with a per-person stimulus and would let
a reactor's asynchrony settle at zero by design. Rh1 and Rh2 are
synchronisation effects, so the study block does not use the lead.
Claim limits: audio_offset_ms (40) is a configured constant, not a
measured latency on the study laptop; measure it once with a
microphone against the trigger event if the thesis wants absolute
asynchrony, otherwise report within-device numbers. The scoring
windows are game feel, not research criteria (rhythm.py). The
rhythm block is the rhythmic auditory cueing arm and the battery
carries no fixed-cadence control block to set against it, so this
mode is a timing measurement here and no RAS-against-control claim
is available (Thaut, McIntosh and Rice 1997, Journal of the
Neurological Sciences 151(2):207-212, is the effect that would need
that control). A study that wants it adds the control block and the
sitting grows.

1.7 Mirror (both hands; NOT PLAYED on the one-board rig)
-------------------------------------------------------
One-board note (24 September 2026): Mirror needs a board on each
hand, so the battery does not play it and M1, M2 and W2 are DROPPED.
The section stays because a two-board rig still runs the mode.
Primary: mirror.mean_gap_ms (absolute right-left onset difference on
clean pairs), n_clean_pairs, hit rate, right_hand_mean_rt_ms and
left_hand_mean_rt_ms (a within-trial asymmetry).
Known effects to reproduce:
  M1 Homologous in-phase pressing is the stable coordination mode
     (Kelso 1984): mean gap well under the 350 ms gate, expected
     under about 60 ms. Descriptive with CI.
  M2 Dominant-hand press leads the non-dominant one: paired RT
     within the same trial, dominant faster (same basis as R2).
Reliability class: unknown for this device; predicted moderate.
Claim limits: the adaptive cadence engine underneath moves the BPM,
so the gap is measured across a changing pace; report bpm_min and
bpm_max alongside. Efficacy of bilateral training is contested and
not claimed (mirror.py).

1.8 Force Pilot (one hand, short form, one climb of the
     twelve-wave ladder, both passes)
-------------------------------------------------------
One-board note (24 September 2026): the block plays the right hand,
twelve runs, in both passes; its hand-level MAE and time in corridor
are T2 and T3 in Section 4.5.
Primary (notebook sec_force_tracking, offline from raw.csv): RMSE
and MAE in percent of max, time in corridor, cross-correlation lag
per wave class, press versus release MAE on the symmetric shapes,
section MAE (hold_in, ramp_up, hold_top, release, sine, assess_sos),
Lodha sub-1 Hz spectral ratio. Block level:
force_pilot.overall.mae_pct, time_in_corridor, release_mae_pct,
press_mae_pct, section_mae_pct (the in-game split by ramp direction
is feedback only; the research number is the offline one).
Scoring notes (25 September 2026, from the first real ladder runs):
  - The zero. Each run is scored from the zero the game read it
    against. The game logs it (ref_counts) from 25 September 2026;
    an older run rebuilds it from the pad reading when the announce
    card opened, and a run straight after one on the same finger
    carries that run's zero. The old rule, the median of the second
    before the run, caught fingers already pressing toward the first
    hold and moved MAE by up to 5.6 percent of max against the
    game's own score; the rebuilt zero agrees with the game to a
    median 0.03 percent. Force is clamped at zero as the game shows
    it. The game no longer re-tares a finger that flies two levels
    in a row (Storm into Uncharted), since it has not rested.
  - The lag. The cross-correlation now scores each lag as a Pearson
    r over the overlapping samples. The old sum put a cusp at zero
    lag and read 0 ms on every ramp wave whatever the true lag (on a
    synthetic Tide: 0 ms for true lags of 50, 150 and 250 ms).
  - Idle runs, where the finger never pressed (95th percentile of
    force under 2 percent of max, when the lowest target is 8), are
    left out like demo runs. Three closed a real block.
  - Press against release reads only the shapes that ask for both
    at one rate: Tide, Hills and both halves of every sine. Dunes is
    reported apart.
Known effects to reproduce:
  F1 Error grows with target bandwidth and unpredictability. Read
     in the battery as the wave ladder's own ordering: the twelve
     waves in force_pilot.py are laid out slowest to fastest, Slow
     breath through Storm, so a level number is a bandwidth, and the
     check is a per-participant Spearman rho of level MAE against
     level number, tested above zero across the cohort. The original
     wording was MAE assess_sos > sine > holds ordered within
     participant; the battery overrides force_pilot to passes: 1,
     one climb of the ladder, and the assessment and sine segments
     belong to a standalone run that this design does not play, so
     that form has no data on a study day and would have stayed
     undecided however many students turned up. The notebook still
     computes the old form when a selection happens to hold those
     segments. Same literature either way (Kurillo 2005 varied
     target shape for exactly this reason; Lodha 2013 puts the
     healthy variability structure below 1 Hz).
  F2 Visuomotor lag positive, of order 100 to 300 ms (a sanity
     check on the corridor timing, not a literature norm). Read on
     the non-periodic waves (Open ocean, Storm, Uncharted), runs
     whose r at the lag is 0.35 or more (Brewer 2009's floor).
     Periodic waves can be flown ahead of time (lag under 20 ms on
     repeated patterns, Day 1984) and ramp and step waves are a few
     edges, so pooling all twelve read prediction as a short lag.
     With 8 s of corridor visible, even the non-periodic lag may sit
     under 100 ms; that is anticipation, not a timing fault, when r
     is high.
  F3 Release error approximately equal to press error in young
     adults. Release-specific impairment is the Parkinson's marker
     (Davidson 2025; Davidson 2026; Chung 2023) and an ageing one
     (Davidson 2024), so a young cohort should show a small
     difference; report the paired difference with its CI as an
     equivalence statement. Read on the symmetric shapes only (Tide,
     Hills, and the rising and falling halves of each sine): Dunes
     releases about three times faster than it climbs, so a normal
     150 ms lag alone would put its release error above its press
     error with no release deficit at all.
  F4 Time in corridor at or above 0.8 on most runs, reported PER
     LEVEL across the climb. 0.8 is force_pilot.py's GREAT_TIC, the
     game's own Great feedback tier, not a promotion criterion:
     force_pilot.py has no staircase, the ladder is fixed, and
     nothing promotes or demotes. The corridor also narrows from a
     half-width of 8 to 4 percent of max (16 to 8 percent wide) up
     the twelve waves, so a single pooled
     median mixes corridor widths; the per-level breakdown is the
     one to read. This row says the corridor is flyable at each
     level for a healthy hand, which is what the ladder needs to be
     usable.
Error versus level IS produced by this battery, through F1 above:
the twelve waves are climbed in a fixed order and a level number is
a bandwidth. What the battery cannot give is a level effect
separated from time on task, because level 12 is always last and
always after 23 runs, and it cannot give a repeated climb. For
either, run a separate level pilot: six participants, one extra sitting,
runs_per_finger 3 with the ladder free, so each finger reaches level
2 or 3 and MAE by level can be plotted. Cheap, and it belongs in the
instrumentation section.
Parkinson's-linked measures (EXPLORATORY, notebook
sec_force_pilot_pd; no hypothesis, descriptive norms for this
device): release against press as a ratio and in time within 5
percent of target (Davidson 2025, 2026); gain and phase per sine
component from a joint least-squares fit (Davidson 2026); signed
error (Jordan 1994); the terminal phase after a release (Davidson
2025); guided rise and release rates as 20 to 80 percent transitions
(Chung 2023; Neely 2013); decrement across beats and cycles (Tinaz
2016; Kang 2010); steadiness on the raised holds beside the pad's own
noise (Tobin 2025; Moritz 2005); pauses on the ramps (Howard 2022;
Naik 2011); lag by wave class; 2 to 8 Hz error power and sample
entropy (Brewer 2009; Vaillancourt 2001); enslaving (Park 2012,
2014). The cohort table carries them as pd_* rows in the normative
table and an exploratory block of the reliability chapter, never in
the paired tests. Formulas and sources:
docs/research/new_modes/force-pilot-parkinsons.md, Table B.
Reliability class: moderate to good for hand-level MAE and time in
corridor (twelve runs per hand per pass); per-finger numbers rest on
two to four runs of different waves, 13 to 16 s each, and are
reported but not given an MDC.
Claim limits: the probe is a maximal flat-finger press on a pad, not
grip or pinch MVC; nothing is reported in newtons (targets and
errors are percent of the finger's own max on the same pad, so the
gain cancels), and the pads' noise and drift are measured and inside
specification (Section 4.8 h); research numbers come from the offline
re-score, never the in-game score.

1.10 Buzz Hunt (one hand, short form: localisation and span only)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
One-board note (24 September 2026): the block plays the right hand,
16 localisation trials and 4 span trials. Chance for a four-finger
localisation is 25 percent; B1 to B5 are unchanged.
Primary: loc.accuracy per hand and per lane at the fixed 150 ms
pulse, the confusion matrix (adjacent-finger error share), catch
false-alarm rate and d-prime, median localisation RT, the top
response-window level reached per hand (window.<hand>.top_level,
levels 3.0, 2.0, 1.5, 1.2 s), span max_correct and novel versus
Hebb accuracy.
2026-09 change, and why the threshold is gone: the localisation
pulse used to run a duration staircase and every correct answer
shortened the buzz until it could not be felt; the 40 ms floor was
the host's, not a perceptual one, and a coin ERM has barely started
moving when a 40 ms command ends (lag about 40 ms, rise about 87 ms,
Precision Microdrives 310-103 class values in config latency.*). The
pulse is now fixed at 150 ms (one firmware hold, inside the 50 to
200 ms band Kaaresoja and Linjama 2005 found usable on a phone
motor) and difficulty moves the response window instead: up a level
after 6 correct of the last 8 trials, down after 2 misses in the
last 4, level stamped on every row. threshold.* in block_summary is
empty unless buzz_hunt.duration_staircase was on for that block.
Known effects to reproduce:
  B1 Localisation near ceiling: accuracy above 90 percent per hand
     at the 150 ms pulse.
  B2 Errors cluster on neighbouring fingers: adjacent share of
     errors above 0.5 (mislocalisations fall on nearer fingers,
     Schweizer, Maier, Braun and Birbaumer 2000, Somatosensory and
     Motor Research 17:309-316; Weber 2023 in buzz_hunt.py for the
     misreferral map).
  B3 Guessing priced: catch false alarms under 10 percent.
  B4 Tactile-only span around 4 items, well below visual span
     (buzz_hunt.py and echo.py both state the ceiling); Hebb trials
     at least as accurate as novel trials.
  B5 The window ladder climbs: a healthy hand plays at level 1 (the
     2.0 s window) or higher inside its 12 trials per hand in the
     battery (16 in free play). A 92 percent responder reaches the
     top in about 22 trials on the headless simulation, so 12 trials
     allow one promotion and often a second; top_level counts the
     highest level a trial was played at, final_level where the
     ladder ended.
Reliability class: poor for accuracy (ceiling restricts range),
moderate for span; the top window level is an ordinal with four
values and 12 trials per hand in the short form, so it is reported
as a distribution and a proportion at each level, never as a mean.
Median RT at the fixed pulse is the continuous localisation number
and is the one an ICC would be computed on, in the study that can
compute one.
Claim limits: ERM lag, rise and stop time are datasheet class
values until scripts/latency_check.py has been run on this rig, so
the gap threshold (not in the battery) is within-device only; the
gap stage is dropped from the battery because at its 120 ms floor
(the motor's spin-down) it cannot reach the healthy temporal
discrimination range (about 30 to 50 ms, Borngraber 2022 in
docs/research/new_modes/carpal-nerve.md); the distractor stage is
dropped for time. For carpal tunnel this suite is measurement, not
therapy (Jerosch-Herold 2016).

1.11 Echo (one 4-lane block on the right hand)
----------------------------------------------
One-board note (24 September 2026): the block plays the right hand's
four lanes, the layout of the retail SIMON toy. E1 reads the four-lane
Simon band: college students reach spans of about seven on four
buttons (Gendle and Ransom 2006, in echo.py), so a cohort median of 5
to 9 is consistent. E3 is DROPPED on the one-board rig: a sequence
past four items holds every lane, so a wrong finger lands on a
sequence lane by construction and an intrusion cannot happen; no
number of participants decides it. The eight-lane text below stands
for a two-board rig.
Primary: span, total_correct, product_score (Kessels total: span x
correct sequences), n_omissions, error taxonomy from the notebook
(transposition, intrusion, omission), Hebb versus novel accuracy by
length.
Known effects to reproduce:
  E1 Cohort span in the region of the Corsi norm: healthy adults
     average 6.2 blocks, SD 1.3 (Kessels et al. 2000, Applied
     Neuropsychology 7:252-258, n = 70). Eight lanes with revisits,
     bimodal presentation and a press response are named
     deviations, so E1 is a plausibility band, not a norm
     comparison; a cohort median of 5 to 8 is consistent.
  E2 DROPPED. It read Hebb repetition learning, hidden-sequence
     trials more accurate than novel trials at matched length (Hebb
     1961; Couture and Tremblay 2007, in echo.py). The hidden repeat
     is a property of the ladder rule: echo.py sets is_hebb only
     when rule == "ladder", and the battery plays the shipped
     default rule: simon, one sequence per game grown by one item a
     trial. No number of participants decides E2 under that rule, so
     it is dropped rather than left waiting for data. Buying it back
     costs a second echo block under the ladder rule, which the 45
     minute sitting has no room for. E1 and E3 are unaffected and
     Echo still has checks.
  E3 Serial-order errors dominate: a wrong finger lands on a lane
     the sequence holds more often than chance. Chance is worked out
     per miss, as the share of the other lanes that the sequence
     holds, and the test is exact (Poisson-binomial, one-sided); its
     p joins the Holm family with the other tested rows.
     Amended 23 September 2026, before any participant: the first
     wording set transpositions against intrusions, which the lane
     count decides on its own (six items on eight lanes hold about
     half the lanes). Omissions carry no lane and are reported, not
     tested.
Reliability class: moderate (span is a coarse integer; the product
score has the better distribution, echo.py).
Claim limits: never read against Kessels or Farrell Pagulayan norms
as a test result; the bilateral block is a bimanual span and is
never pooled with unilateral echo blocks (none are run here).
Choosing the 8-lane block is deliberate: eight distinct locations is
the closest this rig gets to Corsi's nine.

1.12 Cross-mode summary
-----------------------
Strong for a healthy cohort (known effect, expected good
reliability): reaction, rhythm, force pilot, chords (C1 to C3),
echo. Reaction, force pilot and chords are also the three the second
pass replays, so they carry the reliability result. Group-level only: pattern (P1 reliable at the
group level, individual score unreliable by published
meta-analysis).
Ceiling or floor limited: buzz hunt (accuracy ceiling; the window
ladder tops out at level 3), adaptive (bpm cap). Wrong population:
syllables.


2. STUDY DESIGN
===============

2.1 Design and sample
---------------------
ONE session, ONE board, the right-hand device, TWO passes. Every
block is the right hand. The only repeated factor is the pass: pass 1
plays the nine one-hand modes once, pass 2 replays Reaction, Force
Pilot and Chords after the rest. The main hand picked at login, with
the Edinburgh short form on the paper intake sheet as the check, says
whether the device hand is the participant's dominant hand.

Why one board. Only one hand device will be used this semester, and
the printed chassis is the right hand; the mirrored left half exists
only in CAD. A second board would bring back the within-person hand
contrast and Mirror. Without it, the minutes the second hand's blocks
used to take buy the second pass, and the design gains the
within-session test-retest the one-pass version could not compute.

Who takes part. Anyone who volunteers, right- or left-handed: the
supply is whoever turns up. Everyone plays the same right-hand
device, so every pooled number describes the right hand on this rig,
over a sample whose handedness mix is recorded (the main hand at
login, the Edinburgh short form on paper). Pooling everyone is the
primary analysis, amended 24 September 2026 before any participant,
for three reasons: the task, the device and the hand are identical
for all; in healthy young adults the dominant hand's advantage on
these measures is small, and smaller still in left-handers, whose
between-hand differences in tapping are smaller than right-handers'
(Peters and Durding 1979) and whose simple reaction time differs
little between the hands (Nisiyama and Ribeiro-do-Valle 2014); and
turning left-handers into a separate, tiny, undescribed group would
waste their data. Two analyses make the most of the mix instead of
hiding it: the handedness comparison of Section 4.4 (left-handers'
non-dominant hand against right-handers' dominant hand, between
people, with the LQ as a graded version) and the sensitivity rerun of
Section 4.9 (every check on the right-handers alone). Expect about
one participant in ten to be left-handed.

Sample size is set by the day, not by a power calculation. One rig,
one participant at a time, 60 minutes booked each:

  8 hour day  :  8 students
  10 hour day : 10 students
  12 hour day : 12 students

Book more than the target: ten booked usually means eight who turn
up. A second collection day doubles the n and is the cheapest way to
improve every number in the analysis.

What that n supports, written down before the data arrives.

  Normative ranges. With n = 10 the smallest and largest values
  observed sit at about the 9th and 91st percentiles of the
  underlying distribution, because the expected quantile of the i-th
  of n ordered values is i / (n + 1). The 5th and 95th percentiles
  therefore cannot be estimated. Report n, median, IQR, the full
  range and every participant's own point, and give the
  distribution-free interval for the median: at n = 10 it runs from
  the 2nd to the 9th ordered value (97.9 percent coverage), close to
  the whole spread. Saying that plainly is worth more than a
  percentile the data cannot support.

  Reliability. The ICC interval is wide at this n: Bonett (2002)
  sizes a sample for a target interval width and at 8 to 12 people
  that width spans two or three of Koo and Li's bands. In the
  simulated cohort (n = 9 right-handers) ICC(2,1) of 0.91 on
  reaction median RT came with a 95 percent interval of 0.67 to 0.98
  (M, the notebook's exact F interval). So T1 to T5 (Section 4.5)
  are reported against the class Section 1 predicted, not tested,
  and the interval is always printed with the point.

  The pass 2 minus pass 1 shift (R3). A paired equivalence test
  against a 20 ms margin: at n = 10 it concludes equivalence only
  when the shift's interval is tight, which is why the margin and
  the interval are both in the row.

Same rig, same laptop, same config file: record the git commit hash
and the config path on the intake sheet. Both are already in
metadata.json via config_snapshot and software_version.

2.2 Counterbalancing, the rest and fatigue
------------------------------------------
Two mode orders, assigned by code (code mod 4 = 1 or 3: order A;
2 or 0: order B). With one hand there is no hand order left to cross.
The cell is written into every block's metadata (battery.cell). At 8
to 12 participants four to six sit in each order; order is not a
factor anything is tested on and is reported as a description.

  Order A, pass 1: reaction, rhythm, echo, force pilot, chords, buzz
  hunt, pattern, adaptive, syllables.
  Order B, pass 1: force pilot, chords, buzz hunt, adaptive,
  syllables, reaction, rhythm, echo, pattern.
  Pass 2, both orders: reaction, force pilot and chords, in the order
  pass 1 played them (A: reaction, force pilot, chords; B: force
  pilot, chords, reaction), so each mode's gap between its two blocks
  is as even as the order allows.

Pattern is the last block of pass 1 in order B and the seventh in
order A. It is the longest single block and the one whose individual
score is least reliable (Section 1.2). If a session runs over, pattern
is the block to drop and log as missed.

The waits, as the preset ships them. The 60 second stretch note sits
in pass 1 (order A before force pilot; order B before chords,
straight off force pilot's sustained holding). The 3 minute rest is
the gap between the passes: it is the test-retest interval, and it
also gives pass 2 a rested hand to start on. The rest holds the Start
button for its first minute and its length is logged (battery log
rest_taken_s, and battery.rest_before_s in the next block's
metadata), because a block that starts straight after a break starts
fast. Bonstrup et al (2019, Current Biology) read those post-rest
jumps as rapid consolidation; Gupta and Rickard (2022, npj Science of
Learning, N = 159) reproduced the same jumps from a model with no
offline learning in it at all; Das et al (2025, PNAS) showed they
disappear within seconds under equal testing conditions and appear
for random sequences that cannot be learned. The camps disagree about
the mechanism and agree on the practical point: the first block of
pass 2 starts on a fresh hand, so R3 and the reliability of whichever
mode opens pass 2 carry that caveat, printed beside the number. Rest
length itself is not a worry: 15 s, 30 s and self-paced inter-block
rests gave identical implicit sequence learning at n = 268
(Szucs-Bencze et al 2023, eNeuro).

Fatigue over 45 minutes. Sustained-attention performance declines
within the first half hour and a proper rest restores more than a
task switch does (Helton and Russell 2015, Cognition). Micro-breaks
of ten minutes or less reduce fatigue and raise vigour across 22
studies (Albulescu et al 2022, PLOS ONE). Long cognitive test
sessions raise felt fatigue without lowering scores (Ackerman and
Kanfer 2009, JEP Applied, 3.5 to 5.5 h, N = 239), so 45 minutes of
short varied games with one rest is well inside what students
tolerate. Muscle fatigue is still the one to avoid: training at or
beyond fatigue impaired skill acquisition on later days even after
the fatigue had gone (Branscheidt et al 2019, eLife). Hence no block
over about 6.5 minutes, force targets at a fraction of the
participant's own maximum, the force mode's own rests left in, and
the two 0 to 10 tiredness questions on the paper sheet (Section
5.2). Pass 1 is about 24 minutes of play before the rest; the stretch
note breaks it.

One sitting is one draw, not the length of the sitting. Several
blocks draw fresh material every time by design (their seed keys are
empty, so a participant never meets the same sequence twice), and
reaction's exponential foreperiod moves the most. Measured with the
script's --repeats flag, eight sittings on each of six codes (P01 to
P06 over both orders, P04 and P06 left-handed), 48 in all: medians
43.73 to 44.17 minutes, whole range 43.17 to 44.92, none over the 45
minute budget and none near the 50 minute hard stop. Quote the median
and the range, not one run.

Read the headroom honestly: about a minute against the budget. The
model hand is a healthy adult on a good day, so a slower participant
turns the strip amber, and amber means "into the booking's buffer"
rather than "something has gone wrong". The hard stop at 50 is where
the 15 minutes of consent, handedness form, hand measurements and rig
reset stop fitting inside the hour each student is booked for.

What the model leaves out, and what a real session adds: questions, a
redone calibration finger, the odd retried block, and transitions
nearer 20 s than 10. That puts a typical real session near 48
minutes, inside the hard stop and inside the booking.

Trim ladder, if the pilot's median clock exceeds 46 minutes. Apply
in this order and record it in the preset comment:
  1 buzz hunt loc_trials_per_hand 16 to 12 (about 0.4 min);
  2 echo games 2 to 1 (about 1.5 min; it costs the within-block span
    read);
  3 pass 2 drops chords (about 2.8 min; T4 and T5 go with it, so it
    is the last one).
Chords is already at 40 a block in both passes, down from 60, so the
two blocks stay comparable; any change to its counts must be made to
both passes together.

Reference full-form durations, for the thesis to state what was
shortened: reaction 25 trials about 3 min (config comment, M);
pattern 10 takes about 11 min for a healthy adult (A); chords 100
chords about 8.5 min (A); syllables 40 words 8.5 min (M); buzz hunt
one hand 8.5 min (M); force pilot two passes of the ladder about 7
min a hand (A); adaptive 40 trials 30 to 43 s in the recorded test
blocks on disk (M).

Simulated cohort (scripts/simulate_cohort.py): codes play one sitting
each through the real engine and the real one-board Play all, the way
the RA runs it. Each code is a person with fixed traits (base RT,
span, asynchrony, force lag and steadiness, enslaving, reading
accuracy), about one in eight left-handed, and every block draws
fresh trial noise around them, so pass 1 against pass 2 has an
honest amount of person in it for the reliability chapter to
recover. A small within-block warm-up that fades is injected too. The
truth is written to truth.json and the minutes to durations.csv
beside the tree, so an analysis can be checked against what was
injected. It checks the pipeline, not the people: a model participant
cannot stand in for the effects the study exists to measure, and its
ICCs describe the simulator, never the device.

2.4 Short forms and frozen ladders: exact config keys
-----------------------------------------------------
The battery is a protocol preset in config/default.yaml
(protocol.presets.study_battery). The hub's PLAY ALL button (or A)
builds the plan for the logged-in code (the order from the code;
every block on the right hand, the one board renamed to it if Left
was picked at login) and runs it through the engine's
protocol runner, laying the keys below over the live config for the
length of the run and putting them back when the last block ends or
the session ends. Between blocks the results screen shows as usual
and NEXT UP carries the next step, so every block keeps its GET
READY countdown and its own rests; the session strip reads PLAY ALL
k/12 with the running minutes beside it, and the NEXT UP card names
the pass. Each block's metadata.json carries battery.id,
battery.cell, battery.position and battery.phase (pass1 or pass2),
and
config_snapshot shows the short-form keys the block ran under. A
block that follows the scheduled rest also carries
battery.rest_before_s, the rest the participant actually took.
No --config file and no per-participant yaml is needed; a study
laptop only needs session.suggest_code: always so the login offers
P01 on an empty tree (the default, auto, offers a code once one
exists on the machine).

The two waits are preset keys, not code: stretch_s 60 (a suggestion,
the button is never locked), rest_s 180 and rest_min_s 60 (the
button is held for the first minute, then reads "Start early"). An
entry carrying rest_before wins over one carrying stretch_before, so
a card never shows two countdowns.

The keys the preset applies, with the values the design fixed
(tests/test_study_battery.py pins this list exactly, so a scoring,
window or detection key can never creep in through the preset):

  reaction:
    sub_mode: choice
    block_trials: 20              # 25 in the two-visit design
    response_windows_s: [2.0]     # one rung: window never moves
  force_pilot:
    passes: 1                     # one climb of the fixed ladder
  chords:
    subblocks: 2                  # 40 trials, both passes
    trials_per_subblock: 20
    sync_windows_ms: [150]        # one rung: W fixed at 150 ms
  buzz_hunt:
    loc_trials_per_hand: 16       # one hand: 16 localisation trials
    distractor_trials_per_hand: 0
    span_trials: 4                # 8 in the two-visit design
    gap_trials_per_hand: 0
    catch_rate: 0.1
  pattern:
    short_session: true
    soc_cycles_per_block: 3
    random_block_trials: 32
  echo:
    games: 2                      # best of two, and their spread
    max_len: 10                   # ceiling, to hold the 3 min line
  syllables:
    rung: 3                       # the mid rung, played once
    words_per_block: 12
    round_size: 6
    break_s: 10
  game:
    total_trials: 40              # pins the adaptive block length
  rhythm:
    difficulty: medium            # the preset pins the track too
    tactile_mode: on_beat         # the buzz on the beat (Section 1.6)
    The preset pins Easy_Lemon.mp3 (126 s) and the battery opens the
    block on it without the song screen; if the file is missing the
    song screen opens and the RA picks it by hand.
  mirror: not played on the one-board rig.

A mode played in both passes plays the same counts both times, or
its two scores would not be comparable: the preset applies one set of
keys for the whole sitting, so this holds by construction.

Every one of those is a COUNT, a rest or a frozen ladder rung, except
rhythm.tactile_mode, which is a cue condition: it moves when the buzz
arrives, never what a hit is. None of them moves a threshold, a window
or a scoring rule. In the
two-visit design the freeze existed so a person's two goes were the
same task. It matters for two different reasons now: every
participant plays the identical task, so the normative table is not
a mixture of difficulties; and a frozen ladder is what makes the
trial index inside a block mean elapsed time rather than difficulty,
which is what the within-block measure in Section 4.5 rests on.

Everything else runs on the shipped config/default.yaml as it is,
with config/user_settings.yaml left at its defaults on the study
laptop: the cue mix (buzz and sound before the target, nothing
after, target shown), test mode off, balanced targets, reports on,
EEG off, quick calibration on, the one board auto-assigned and put
on the right hand by PLAY ALL. The preset does not write these; the run sheet
checks them and every block's config_snapshot records them. The
intake fields the software does not hold (affected side, impairment
score) stay empty for a study code; the cohort is identified by the
code prefix and by battery.id in every battery block's metadata, and
"healthy control" is written on the intake sheet, not into
metadata.json.

Two ladders still move inside their own block, both deliberately and
both reported. Buzz Hunt's response-window ladder promotes on six
correct of the last eight, so a block that starts at the 3.0 s
window can end below it. Echo's sequence lengthens as the
participant succeeds. Neither is a defect: they are how those two
modes measure. They are the reason both need special handling in the
within-block measure (Section 4.5b), because in those two blocks a
later trial is a harder trial. Echo's Hebb sequence and Buzz Hunt's
Hebb span are seeded from the participant code, which with one block
each means every participant meets their repeating sequence the same
number of times.

Ladder freezes were checked against the code: force_pilot climbs its
fixed 12-level wave ladder once; chords and reaction take the window
ladder as a list and clamp the level to its length, so a one-entry
list is a fixed window. Each is covered by a regression test through
the real engine (tests/test_study_battery.py, ShortFormTests).

Statistical cost of each short form:

  Force Pilot, one pass of the ladder, 12 runs on the one hand, in
  each pass. Per-finger metrics rest on a single run per level, so
  per-finger values are cohort-pooled and the hand-level mean is the
  unit (and the T2 and T3 metric).

  Chords, 40 trials a block in each pass (32 chords across the 11
  chord types, and 8 single fingers, two per finger), so per-chord
  and per-single cells are cohort-pooled. Both sub-blocks are within-hand,
  which is what makes the start-to-end comparison in Section 4.5b
  possible.

  Buzz Hunt, 16 localisation trials and 4 span trials, no gap or
  distractor stage. The window ladder allows at most one or two
  promotions in 16 trials and the top level is censored, so the
  notebook reports the level distribution rather than a mean. Four
  localisation trials per finger, so the confusion matrix is
  cohort-pooled.

  Pattern, 36-press takes and a 32-trial random take, once. The
  first cycle of every take is trimmed by the mode's RT hygiene,
  leaving 24 analysable presses per take against 48 in the full
  form, so the probe-minus-flanker standard error grows by about
  1.4. There is no second pattern block, so there is no retention
  probe of any kind: the learning score is measured inside the one
  block and nowhere else.

  Reaction, 20 trials a block, in each pass, window fixed at 2.0 s.
  The fixed
  window costs nothing (a healthy adult almost never exceeds 1.2 s).
  The trial count costs twice over now: a split-half inside a
  20-trial block is estimated from two halves of ten, which is why
  the notebook uses a permutation split-half over 1000 random splits
  and prints its interval rather than one Spearman-Brown number; and
  a within-block trend has only 20 points to sit on. If the pilot
  clock has room, this is the first rung to give back (Section 2.3).

  Syllables and adaptive, one block each, in pass 1. Two ceiling
  checks each (A5, A6, S6, S7) and no within-block check. They are
  in the session so that every one-hand mode is played and so the
  sitting changes pace.

4.3 Descriptives and normative ranges
-------------------------------------
Per metric and hand_role: n, median, IQR, minimum, maximum, and the
distribution-free interval for the median (the ordered values whose
coverage is nearest 95 percent, with the coverage printed beside
them). Mean and SD as well where the values are not obviously
skewed, because the literature values a reader will compare against
are usually means.

Percentiles: the 5th and 95th are NOT reported unless n reaches 20.
Below that the table prints the range and a note that the extremes
of a sample of n sit near the i / (n + 1) quantiles, so the smallest
and largest of ten observations are about the 9th and 91st
percentiles (Section 2.1). This is the normative table the thesis
prints, and its honesty about width is part of the result.

Plots at this n are dot strips, one point per participant, not
boxes: a box plot of ten points hides the ten points and invents a
shape. The known-effect reference (for example Kessels 6.2 for span)
is drawn as a line where one exists.

4.4 Dominant versus non-dominant
--------------------------------
One-board rig (24 September 2026). Nobody plays both hands, so there
is no pair to test and R2, C5, Rh3 and M2 are DROPPED. Handedness
becomes a between-person factor: the right-handers play their
dominant hand and the left-handers their non-dominant hand, on the
same device. Per metric, first pass: n and median per group; the
median shift (left minus right) with a percentile bootstrap interval,
each group resampled on its own; Cliff's delta; and a two-sided
Mann-Whitney p. All of it is EXPLORATORY: no direction is predicted,
no multiplicity control covers it, and a group needs three people
before anything but medians prints. The expected answer is a small
or no difference (Peters and Durding 1979; Nisiyama and
Ribeiro-do-Valle 2014), so a large one on a timing measure would be
the thing to discuss, with the caveat that a handful of left-handers
also differ from the right-handers in every other way those few
people differ.

The graded look: where the Edinburgh laterality quotient is recorded
(the short form on paper, typed into sessions/intake_sheet.csv, one
row per code, columns participant and edinburgh_lq), Spearman rho of
LQ against each pooled metric over everyone, from five participants
up. Also exploratory. LQ runs from -100 (fully left) to +100 (fully
right); the short form's four items are writing, throwing, toothbrush
and spoon (Veale 2014).

The rest of this section is the two-board analysis, which the
notebook still runs on a two-board tree. Per metric with a hand_role
pair. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is
the primary at this n, with the matched-pairs rank-biserial
correlation as its effect size, because ten differences are not
enough to check normality with any confidence. Reported beside it:
the paired difference (dominant minus non-dominant) with a 95
percent CI, the paired t, Cohen dz = mean difference / SD of
differences, and Shapiro-Wilk on the differences as a description
rather than a gate. Report both tests when they disagree, and never
report only the one that gave the smaller p.

Bootstrap intervals (10000 resamples, seeded, the notebook already
has BOOT_N and BOOT_SEED) are printed with the n next to them. A
bootstrap from ten points is a coarse instrument: the resampled
median can only land on the values actually observed, so the
interval is lumpy and its ends are not trustworthy to the decimal.

Directional hypotheses from Section 1 (R2, M2) are one-sided;
everything else two-sided with no claim beyond the interval.

Wilcoxon is primary at every n, not only at this one. Letting the
Shapiro p choose between the Wilcoxon p and the paired t p is a gate
in everything but name, and a data-dependent choice between two p
values is a soft version of reporting the smaller one, which the
paragraph above forbids. At ten participants the choice never bit,
because n under 20 already picked Wilcoxon; a second collection day
takes n past 20 and it would start choosing.

Multiplicity. A cohort run produces dozens of p values, and until
2026-09 none of them was adjusted. The controlled family is the
known-effect and within-block table; nothing else carries a p that
may be quoted as a finding (on a two-board tree the
dominant-versus-non-dominant table adds R2 and M2 as a second planned
family):

  - The known-effect and within-block table (Section 4.6). Holm's
    step-down correction (Holm, S. 1979, Scandinavian Journal of
    Statistics 6(2), 65-70) is applied across the pre-specified rows
    that carry a test. Holm controls the family-wise error rate
    without assuming independence, which these rows do not have: the
    same ten people give every one of them. A row that passes on the
    unadjusted p and not after Holm is reported as "direction only".

A check that could not be run is not a failed check. Where every
pair was identical, or a rank correlation had no variation to
correlate, the verdict word is "no variation", counted apart from
"fail". Six of one run's thirteen "fails" were rows of that kind.

4.5 What can honestly be said about repeatability and progress
--------------------------------------------------------------
Three modes are measured twice, in one sitting, about twenty minutes
apart; the other six once. That gives one honest reliability number
for three modes, (c) below, and rules out the rest, and the plan is
written so no reader can mistake one for the other.

(a) Internal consistency inside a block. For every metric that can
be recomputed from a subset of a block's trials, split the block's
scorable trials into two halves at random, compute the metric on
each half, correlate the halves across participants, and apply the
Spearman-Brown correction. Do it over 1000 random splits (seeded)
and report the median and the 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles rather
than one odd-even number: with 20 reaction trials a single split is
noisy, and the permutation interval says how noisy. Label it
INTERNAL CONSISTENCY everywhere, never reliability without the
qualifier. It says the trials inside one block agree with each
other. It says nothing about whether the same hand would score the
same tomorrow, or in an hour, because nothing at all changes between
the two halves.

No SEM and no MDC95 from a split-half. The arithmetic would run (SEM
= SD x sqrt(1 - r) takes any reliability coefficient), and the result
would be wrong in a direction that matters: a split-half r ignores
every source of variance that acts between administrations, which is
most of the measurement error a patient study cares about, so the SEM
would be too small and the MDC too tight. A too-tight MDC calls noise
a real change, which is the one failure mode this device must not
have. The within-session MDC95 of (c) is too tight for the same
reason, one step less so. So sessions/cohort_results/progress_mdc.yaml
is not written, and the app's TODAY panel shows a direction and a
number and never the word "real". A between-day test-retest study
writes that file.

(b) Within-block improvement, first pass. It is what lets a
participant see themselves getting better during the sitting, and it
is a weaker claim than a repeated block. Both halves of that sentence
go in the thesis.

Definition, per participant, hand, mode and metric with a per-trial
series (the fine series in Section 4.7 figure 4):

  - First against last. Compute the metric on the first third of the
    block's scorable trials and again on the last third. The
    difference is the within-block change. Not computed when the
    block has fewer than 8 scorable trials, and the thirds are
    reported with their trial counts.
  - Slope. Fit value against log(trial index) per participant and
    report the slope and its interval; where a series has at least 8
    points, also fit an exponential (asymptote plus amplitude times
    exp(-rate x index)) and report the rate. Fits are PER PERSON and
    the cohort figure is a distribution of slopes, never a curve
    fitted to the group mean: a power law and an exponential are
    hard to tell apart in averaged data, and averaging across people
    can manufacture a smooth power curve out of individually
    exponential ones (Heathcote, Brown and Mewhort 2000, Psychonomic
    Bulletin and Review).
  - Cohort: median slope with its IQR, the Wilcoxon signed-rank
    against zero, and the count of participants improving out of n.

Which modes it is interpretable for, and which it is not. The
question in every case is whether a later trial is a harder trial.

  Clean, because the task is frozen for the whole block:
    reaction (window fixed, foreperiods drawn at random),
    mirror (two-board rig only),
    pattern (this IS P1: the probe rebound is a within-block
      learning score and the mode already computes it),
    syllables (accuracy per round, descriptive only).
  Usable with the structure named:
    chords, sub-block 1 against sub-block 2, both within-hand and
      both 20 trials,
    rhythm, asynchrony SD per 30 s window of a fixed track: window k
      is the same material for every participant, so windows are
      compared across people at the same k, and a within-person
      trend across windows carries the track's own structure with
      it.
  Not interpretable as improvement:
    echo, because the sequence lengthens as the participant
      succeeds, so trial index is difficulty,
    force pilot, because the 12-level wave ladder is climbed once,
      so MAE against trial is MAE against difficulty; fit MAE
      against level and read the residual trend, and say plainly
      that 12 runs a hand is a short series to read a residual from,
    buzz hunt, because the response window can promote inside the
      block; restrict the RT trend to trials at the starting window
      or carry the level as a covariate,
    adaptive, because BPM moves by design.

The limitation, stated in the notebook output, the figure captions
and the thesis in these terms:

  A within-block slope mixes improvement with warm-up. The first
  trials of any block are also where the participant is still
  working out what the game wants, so part of any early gain is
  instructions landing rather than motor performance improving.
  It cannot be separated from fatigue pulling the other way inside
  the same series. It has no noise floor of its own, so no
  individual's slope can be called real and only the cohort
  direction is claimable. And it is a change in performance during
  practice, not learning that survives (Kantak and Winstein 2012).
  The second pass separates part of it for three modes (R3 and the
  pass 2 minus pass 1 shift in (c)); a delayed retention test is
  still the next study's.

The reaction block is the calibration for all of it. Reaction is the
mode expected not to improve inside its own block, so whatever trend
it shows is the study's own estimate of warm-up and drift, and every
other mode's within-block trend is read against it rather than
against zero.

(c) Within-session test-retest, pass 1 against pass 2, pre-specified
24 September 2026 before any participant. Reaction, force pilot and
chords are replayed after the rest with the same counts and the same
frozen ladders. Per metric, over the pooled sample (everyone; the
right-handers alone in the sensitivity rerun, Section 4.9):

  ICC(2,1), two-way random, absolute agreement, single measure, the
  form Koo and Li (2016) give for test-retest, with its exact F
  interval (McGraw and Wong 1996), and ICC(3,1) beside it. Read on
  Koo and Li's bands: under 0.5 poor, 0.5 to 0.75 moderate, 0.75 to
  0.9 good, above 0.9 excellent.
  SEM = SD x sqrt(1 - ICC(2,1)), SD pooled over the two passes, and
  MDC95 = 1.96 x sqrt(2) x SEM (Weir 2005).
  Bland-Altman: the mean pass 2 minus pass 1 difference and its 95
  percent limits of agreement (Bland and Altman 1986), with a plot.
  The shift: pass 2 minus pass 1 with its paired interval.

The rows, each read against the class Section 1 predicted:

  T1  reaction median_rt_ms        good to excellent (0.75 and up)
  T2  force_pilot mae_pct          moderate to good (0.5 to 0.9)
  T3  force_pilot time_in_corridor moderate to good (0.5 to 0.9)
  T4  chords median_er             moderate (0.5 to 0.75)
  T5  chords median_span_ms        good (0.75 to 0.9)

Reported, not tested: at n = 8 to 12 an ICC interval spans two or
three bands, so the table says whether the point estimate sits in
the predicted band and prints the interval beside it. R3 in Section
4.6 is the one tested claim that reads pass 2.

What it is, in the thesis's own words: within-session reliability,
twenty minutes apart, same seat, same calibration. It leaves out
every source of variance that acts between days (sleep, strap
position, a new calibration, a different room temperature), so the
ICC is an upper bound on day-to-day reliability and the MDC95 a lower
bound on a real change between sessions. It covers three of the nine
modes. Between-day test-retest for all of them is the next study's
first job.

(d) Still dropped, with the reason, so a reader looking for them
stops looking: the pre-to-post change with its responder count; the
first-trials-after-rest against last-trials-before-rest contrast;
the pre-registered learning checks L1 to L8; and any reliability
number for the six modes played once.

  W2 mirror      the right-minus-left onset gap narrows. DROPPED on
                 the one-board rig: Mirror is not played.
  W3 pattern     P1 itself: probe minus flanking trained takes above
                 zero at the cohort level.
  W4 chords      sub-block 2 tighter than sub-block 1 (median ER
                 down, clean hit rate up), both within-hand.
  W5 buzz hunt   localisation RT falls, at a fixed window level.
  W6 force pilot DROPPED. It asked for MAE against run order once
                 the corridor level was regressed out. The battery
                 plays force pilot with passes: 1, so the ladder is
                 climbed once per hand in the same fixed order and
                 level correlates with run index at r = 0.9974.
                 Ordinary least squares residuals are orthogonal to
                 the regressor, so the residual slope is zero BY
                 CONSTRUCTION and no number of participants changes
                 it: measured per block, 3.5e-05, -8.7e-06 and
                 -4.3e-06, printed as a failed check. A both-hands
                 block does fly every level twice, but the second run
                 is the OTHER hand in a fixed order, so that contrast
                 is left minus right and not a within-block trend
                 either. F1 and the force tracking chapter are
                 unaffected. A passes >= 2 block would give the row
                 back, and the notebook computes it when one exists.

Rhythm, echo, adaptive and syllables get no within-block check. In
echo and adaptive a later trial is a harder trial by design, in
rhythm the track's own structure moves under the window, and
syllables has two rounds to its name and is descriptive only
(Section 4.5b). Rhythm and syllables are therefore read as
DESCRIPTIVE in the notebook's within-block chapter: their trend is
printed with its numbers and they are out of the improvement set,
the effect-size forest and the anchor comparison. No other
inferential test is run; everything else is descriptive with
intervals.

The anchor comparison only subtracts like from like. W1 is in
milliseconds per log trial, and a mode whose series is an error
ratio, an accuracy fraction or a millisecond standard deviation is
compared with it as a share of that mode's own first-third median
instead, which is dimensionless. Subtracting a millisecond from a
ratio and printing the result as a number to read is not a
comparison.

The first-third and last-third values, and the per-person slopes,
are taken from the RAW scorable series. Smoothing is for the drawn
curve only: a five-point rolling median makes adjacent points
dependent, which an ordinary least squares standard error assumes
they are not, and on fifteen reaction blocks it moved the per-block
contrast by 8.84 ms on average and one block from a 48 ms gain to
exactly zero.

These are construct checks: the game reproduces what the literature
predicts, which is evidence the measurement is of the right thing.
They are not criterion validity against a clinical measure, which a
healthy cohort cannot give.

5. PARTICIPANTS, SAFETY AND RUN SHEET
=====================================

5.1 Participants, safety and consent
------------------------------------
Risk: the only foreseeable risk is discomfort, which is what the
NHMRC National Statement calls low risk (NHMRC, National Statement
on Ethical Conduct in Human Research, 2023 update, Section 2.1). The
thesis's ethics and professional discussion can describe the study
that way.

Participants: healthy student volunteers, adults 18 and over, right-
or left-handed. Inclusion: normal or corrected vision, able to sit at
a laptop for an hour with a break. Exclusion: current
hand, wrist or forearm injury or pain; diagnosed neurological or
musculoskeletal condition affecting the hands; skin condition or
open wound on the hands; known sensitivity to vibration.

Procedure, in plain words: one visit of about an hour, with a three
minute break and a stretch break in it; the right hand rests flat on
a 3D printed frame with a small force pad under each fingertip; short
games on a laptop screen, three of them played a second time after
the break; light presses only; small vibration motors buzz a
fingertip for a quarter of a second as a cue; a ruler measurement of
the right hand; four questions on hand preference; age and sex. One
visit only, with no follow-up session.

Foreseeable discomforts and their controls:
  Vibration: eccentric-rotating-mass motors at fixed drive, 250 ms
    bursts, felt as a phone-style buzz. No heat, no electrical
    contact with the skin (the motor is inside the frame). Anyone
    who finds it unpleasant can stop; buzz_before can be turned off
    for that participant and the change is logged in cue_flags and
    the intake sheet.
  Sustained light pressing: targets are at most 40 percent of the
    participant's own comfortable maximum press, holds are 16 s,
    runs under 30 s, with rests built in and every rest skippable
    only by the participant's choice. Mild finger fatigue is the
    expected worst case, and at about 33 minutes of play on one hand
    it is a smaller exposure than the designs this replaced.
  Skin contact: pads and frame wiped with 70 percent isopropyl
    between participants; participants wash or sanitise hands on
    arrival; disposable finger cots offered. On a collection day
    this happens 8 to 12 times, so the wipes and cots are stocked
    for the whole day, not for one session.
  Electrical safety: the boards run at 5 V from the laptop USB
    port; no mains-powered part touches the participant.
  Screen and sound: 60 Hz display with no flashing patterns; laptop
    speaker at a moderate fixed volume set once and recorded.
  Boredom or frustration: games are short and scored; participants
    may skip a block or stop at any time, no reason needed.

Data: participant code only in every file the software writes; age,
sex, handedness and hand size on the intake sheet keyed by code; the
consent log is the only link between name and code and is destroyed
at study close. Recorded data are trial tables, 200 Hz force traces
and configuration snapshots; none is identifying. Storage on
Curtin-approved storage, not personal cloud folders; retention per
Curtin's research data policy; the software repository is public on
GitHub and holds no participant data (sessions/ is gitignored).
Participants may withdraw their data until analysis begins.

The information sheet and the debrief say: no deception beyond the
standard "some games repeat a hidden pattern you are not told about"
(pattern, buzz hunt, echo), explained in the debrief; no clinical
assessment or feedback is given; the device is a research prototype,
not a medical device; one session only, and nobody is contacted
again after the day. The study-day kit (docs/study_day/) has the
information sheet, the consent form, the intake sheet and the
debrief to print.

5.2 Research-assistant run sheet (one session)
----------------------------------------------
The printable version, with the information sheet, consent form,
intake sheet (Edinburgh short form included), debrief and the
end-of-day steps, is docs/study_day/. This section is the reasoning
behind it.

One collection day, one participant at a time, 60 minutes booked
each. The 15 minutes off the clock covers consent, the handedness
form, the hand measurements and resetting the rig from the last
person.

BEFORE THE FIRST SESSION OF THE DAY (10 min)
  [ ] Laptop on mains power, app launched as shipped; the login
      screen suggests the next free code (Pxx).
  [ ] Plug in the one board, fitted in the right-hand chassis. It
      self-tests with a short buzz. Nothing else is plugged in.
  [ ] Pads and frame wiped. Finger cots, ruler, intake sheets and
      EHI forms for the whole day on the desk. Speaker at the study
      volume. Water on the desk for the breaks.

BEFORE EACH SESSION (5 min, inside the 15)
  [ ] Wipe pads and frame. New intake sheet: date, code, cell (A/B,
      from the code), commit hash, session start time, caffeine in the
      last two hours (yes/no), hours of sleep last night.

ARRIVAL (15 min, off the clock)
  [ ] Consent, EHI short form, hand length and breadth of the right
      hand, code assigned. A left-hander may take part: tick
      "left-handed, device on the non-dominant hand" on the sheet.
  [ ] Hands washed. Seat height so forearms rest flat, fingers index
      to little on the four pads, thumbs off the frame.
  [ ] Say once: "This runs about three quarters of an hour, all on
      your right hand, with a stretch early on and a proper break
      later, after which three of the games come round again. Press
      lightly, like typing. Some games have a hidden rule; do not
      try to work it out, just play." Never mention sequences or
      patterns again.
  [ ] Say once, and only once: "After each game the screen shows how
      your last few goes in that game compared with your first few."
      Do not repeat it, do not comment on the numbers during the
      session, and do not encourage between blocks: every
      participant has to meet the same RA.

CLOCK STARTS (target 45 min, hard stop 50)
  [ ] Check the login: code, age, sex, MAIN HAND (Left or Right,
      required), hand length and breadth. The EHI score stays on the
      sheet. LOG IN, then pick RIGHT HAND on the hand screen. Quick
      calibration runs: hand off, hand resting, then a light press
      per finger. If a finger fails, redo once, then continue and
      note it. (Picking Left by mistake is not fatal: PLAY ALL puts
      the board on the right hand and calibrates it first.)
  [ ] Press PLAY ALL on the hub (or A). The app plays the order
      below; after each block press Start on the NEXT UP card (or
      N). The card names the step and the pass; the strip reads PLAY
      ALL k/12 with the minutes beside it, amber past the target and
      red past the hard stop. If the board drops between blocks the
      card says "Waiting for the board" and the step stays put: plug
      it back in and press Start. A block that
      cannot be run at all is skipped from the hub with Skip step
      (S), which asks first, and the reason written on the sheet; a
      block ended early is offered again.
  [ ] If the app closes mid-sitting, open it, log in with the same
      code and main hand, and press PLAY ALL: blocks finished today
      are not played again.

  Every block is the right hand.

  Order A (P01, P03, P05, ...)     Order B (P02, P04, P06, ...)
  Pass 1                           Pass 1
   1 Reaction                       1 Force Pilot
   2 Rhythm, Easy_Lemon, Medium     2 (60 s stretch)
   3 Echo                             Chords
   4 (60 s stretch)                 3 Buzz Hunt
     Force Pilot                    4 Adaptive
   5 Chords                         5 Syllables
   6 Buzz Hunt                      6 Reaction
   7 Pattern                        7 Rhythm, Easy_Lemon, Medium
   8 Adaptive                       8 Echo
   9 Syllables                      9 Pattern
  (REST, 3 min)                    (REST, 3 min)
  Pass 2                           Pass 2
  10 Reaction                      10 Force Pilot
  11 Force Pilot                   11 Chords
  12 Chords                        12 Reaction

  Per block: read the on-screen card, confirm the participant is
  ready, start. Do not coach during a block. Let the participant
  skip a mode's own internal rests with Space only when they say
  they are ready. Force Pilot and Buzz Hunt refuse to start without
  the board; if it has dropped, reconnect and note the block. Pass 2
  is the same three games again: do not say they are repeats, and
  do not mention the first scores.
  [ ] Tick each block on the sheet as it ends; write the reason for
      any early end or skip.

  AT THE REST AND AT THE END
  [ ] Hands off the pads. Offer water. Do not talk about the games,
      the numbers or how it is going.
  [ ] Ask the two questions at the 3 minute rest, between the
      passes, and again at the end. Write the answers on the sheet
      exactly as worded, 0 = not at all, 10 = completely:
        "Tired in the head?"   0 to 10
        "Tired in the hands?"  0 to 10
      Two sets per session. Note the clock time next to each. The
      60 second stretch is too short to stop for questions.
  [ ] The Start button is held for the first minute of the rest and
      then reads "Start early". Take the full three minutes unless
      the participant asks to go on; write down when the rest was
      cut short. The rest is the gap the reliability numbers are
      measured across, so a cut rest is always written down.

  [ ] At the hard stop: let the current block finish, stop, record
      what was missed. Pattern is the block to lose.

END
  [ ] The last block's screen shows the TODAY table. Let the
      participant read it. Answer questions about their own numbers
      only. Say plainly that it compares the start of each game with
      the end of the same game, that a small change is not
      necessarily a real one, and that no comparison with other
      people is being shown or made.
  [ ] End the session from the hub (confirm the session-end
      warning). Check sessions/<today>/ holds one folder per block
      played, each with trials.csv, raw.csv and metadata.json, and
      that battery.position runs 1 to 12.
  [ ] Debrief: hidden sequences explained, questions answered,
      thanks. Say that the session shows what a
      normal hand does on the device and how performance moves
      within a few minutes of practice, and that it does not show
      what is kept overnight.
  [ ] Type each code's Edinburgh LQ into sessions/intake_sheet.csv
      (participant, edinburgh_lq). Copy today's session folders to the
      approved storage at the end of the day; wipe pads and frame;
      unplug the board.
