HEALTHY BASELINE STUDY
Design for the psychology-student cohort, Thesis Project A

Status: 2026-09-02. The software side is built and tested (the
intake, the battery preset, the cohort chapter of the notebook, the
headless timing script and a simulated cohort run through the real
engine; Sections 2.3, 2.4 and 4.8 say what each one showed). No
participant has been run: every number about people below is a
prediction or a literature figure, and every duration is either
measured headless (marked M) or arithmetic from the shipped config
(marked A). Every literature number is cited; numbers that could not
be verified are marked as such and are not load-bearing.

Why this study exists. No patients are available this semester. The
thesis still needs a results chapter with real hands on the device,
so the rig is turned into a measurement instrument and characterised
on healthy adults: what does each game measure in a normal hand, how
repeatable is it a week later, and does it reproduce the effects the
literature says a healthy hand shows. That is a normative and
psychometric study, not a treatment study, and it is a legitimate
thesis result on its own: a device whose numbers are stable and
reproduce known effects is the precondition for any later patient
work, and a device whose numbers are not stable tells the next
student what to fix.

What this study cannot show, and the thesis must say so in the same
breath. A healthy cohort says nothing about sensitivity to
impairment (whether a stroke or carpal tunnel hand scores
differently), nothing about responsiveness (whether the numbers move
with recovery), nothing about the 65 to 80 percent challenge band or
any other therapy claim, and nothing about clinical validity against
an accepted assessment. The normative table is a description of 28
young, mostly right-handed university students on this rig, not a
population norm, and it is read against literature values as an
order-of-magnitude plausibility check only. Test-retest reliability
in a stable healthy hand is an upper bound on what a changing
patient hand would show. The known-effect checks in Section 4.6 are
construct checks: the game reproduces an effect the literature
predicts, which is evidence the measurement is of the right thing,
not evidence the device is a valid clinical instrument.


DECISIONS IN ONE SCREEN
=======================

- Within-subject, both hands, N = 28 analysed (recruit 32), two
  visits 7 days apart (window 5 to 9), same time of day within 2 h.
- Per-visit battery of ten blocks, 50 minutes including login,
  calibration and rests. Identical at both visits.
- Nine modes in the battery. Syllables is excluded (paediatric).
  Adaptive is excluded (ceiling at bpm_max in a healthy hand, and a
  40-trial block is too short for the controller to converge; the
  controller check stays headless).
- Two mode orders (A, B) crossed with two hand orders, assigned by
  participant code; each participant keeps their cell at both visits.
- Pattern runs on the dominant hand only, last in every battery, in
  its short form. Echo runs one bilateral 8-lane block. Every other
  mode runs both hands.
- Every difficulty ladder that would otherwise move mid-block is
  frozen by config so visit 1 and visit 2 are the same task.
- Participants are P01 to P32. The code goes in the login NAME field
  (the login suggests the next free code off the sessions tree, so
  it is never mistyped) and keys the sessions tree, the index, the
  notebook and the hidden sequences. Names live only in the consent
  log.
- Primary outputs: normative table (median, IQR, 5th to 95th), paired
  dominant versus non-dominant with Cohen dz, ICC(2,1) with 95
  percent CI plus SEM and MDC95, practice effect visit 1 to 2, and
  one pre-specified known-effect check per mode. One tidy CSV.


1. WHAT A HEALTHY COHORT CAN PROVE, PER MODE
============================================

Format per mode: primary metric(s) and where they come from; the
known healthy-adult effect the game must reproduce; expected
test-retest reliability class; honest claim limits. "block_stats"
means the mode's own block_stats() dict, folded into
metadata.json under block_summary.<mode>; "notebook" means the
offline re-score in analysis/session_analysis.ipynb.

Reliability classes use Koo and Li (2016): below 0.5 poor, 0.5 to
0.75 moderate, 0.75 to 0.9 good, above 0.9 excellent, and are
predictions to be tested, not promises.

1.1 Reaction (choice, one hand per block)
-----------------------------------------
Primary: median_rt_ms, lapse_like_rate, n_false_start_total /
n_attempts, accuracy (block_stats.reaction). Secondary: sd_rt_ms,
p10_rt_ms, slope_rt_ms_per_trial, spearman_rho_rt_vs_fp.
Known effects to reproduce:
  R1 The exponential foreperiod works: cohort median of the
     Spearman rho between RT and foreperiod near zero (|rho| under
     0.2) and false starts under 10 percent of attempts. This is the
     anticipation control the mode was built for (Niemi and Naatanen
     1981; Naatanen 1971, both cited in reaction.py).
  R2 Dominant hand faster than non-dominant. The dominant advantage
     is well replicated for tapping speed and consistency (Peters
     1980; Hubel et al. 2013) and is small for reaction time, so R2
     is a directional check, not a powered primary outcome.
  R3 No practice effect on median RT across visits. The PVT family
     the block is modelled on shows no change in median RT or lapses
     over 16 repeated administrations (Basner et al. 2017, Sleep).
Reliability class: good to excellent for median RT (the PVT
tradition; the block is 25 scorable trials, near the validated 3 min
PVT-B of Basner, Mollicone and Dinges 2011).
Claim limits: absolute RTs are device-specific (60 Hz display, force
threshold clock, unmeasured panel latency; Woods 2015 and Plant and
Turner 2009 in reaction.py) and measured to an audio-tactile-visual
cue under the shipped cue defaults. Report them as this device's
numbers, never against published button-press norms. Simple RT is
not run (time), so the choice-versus-simple contrast is not
available.

1.2 Pattern, SRTT (dominant hand only, short form)
--------------------------------------------------
Primary: session_learning_score_ms (probe minus flanking trained
takes, block_stats.pattern.probe_scores) and accuracy_rebound_pct.
Secondary: per-take mean RT curve, random-take baseline RT.
Known effects to reproduce:
  P1 Positive learning score at visit 1 (cohort mean above zero).
     The probe rebound is one of the most replicated effects in
     cognitive psychology (Nissen and Bullemer 1987: 327 to 163 ms;
     Kal et al. 2016 meta-analysis: 69 ms mean rebound in healthy
     and less-affected hands, cited in pattern.py). With the short
     form's smaller exposure expect tens of ms, not 69.
  P2 Larger learning score at visit 2 than visit 1. The trained
     sequence is seeded from the participant code, so visit 2 is a
     retention session; distribution across days improves sequence
     memory (Savion-Lemieux and Penhune 2005, cited in pattern.py).
  P3 Accuracy drops on probe takes (rebound above zero).
Reliability class: POOR for the individual learning score, and this
must be said in the thesis before the data comes in. The 2023
meta-analysis of SRTT test-retest correlations pooled r = 0.28 (95
percent CI 0.16 to 0.40; Oliveira, Hayiou-Thomas and Henderson
2023, Royal Society Open Science), with no moderator rescuing it.
The group-level effect is reliable; the individual score is not a
trait. Report the ICC, expect it to be low, and do not compute an
MDC for it.
Claim limits: dominant hand only, so no asymmetry. Single-session
rebounds can be temporary adaptation (Trofimova 2020 in
pattern.py); P2 is the memory claim. Never tell participants a
sequence exists (Boyd and Winstein, in pattern.py).

1.3 Chords (both hands, short form)
-----------------------------------
Primary: median_er (enslaving ratio, quiet-finger leak over target
press), median_span_ms (chord togetherness), clean hit rate
(chord_outcome_classes.hit / all), per-hand versions in per_hand;
cross-hand: hit_rate_mirror, hit_rate_nonmirror, median_lag_ms,
bilateral_deficit.
Known effects to reproduce:
  C1 Cohort median ER under 0.15. Healthy hands leak roughly 5 to 15
     percent of instructed force at light effort (Abolins et al.
     2020, cited in chords.py: 8 to 10 percent at about 25 percent
     MVC).
  C2 The computed difficulty rank D predicts outcome: negative
     Spearman correlation between D and per-chord hit rate, positive
     between D and per-chord median ER, on the cohort-pooled
     per-chord table. This tests the adjacency rule (enslaving
     largest between neighbours, Zatsiorsky, Li and Latash 2000).
  C3 Ring finger the most enslaved: in the cohort-mean
     chord-conditioned matrix the ring column carries the largest
     leak (Hager-Ross and Schieber 2000; Chiang 2004, in chords.py).
  C4 Cross-hand mirror chords hit more often and land tighter than
     non-mirror chords (the symmetry advantage: Kelso 1984; Haken,
     Kelso and Bunz 1985; Mechsner 2001, in chords.py).
  C5 Dominant versus non-dominant ER: no strong prediction. Force
     coordination studies in young adults often find no dominance
     difference (Scientific Reports 2025, "Comparable neural and
     behavioural performance in dominant and non-dominant hands
     during grasping tasks"), so report the paired result with its
     CI and treat a null as expected.
Reliability class: moderate for ER (a ratio of two noisy peaks
normalised by that day's calibration), good for span_ms.
Claim limits: ER and the matrices are device- and posture-specific
and chord-conditioned (an upper bound on the single-finger cell);
literature values are order-of-magnitude context only. The short
form gives about 20 within-hand chords per hand per visit, so
per-chord cells are cohort-pooled, never per person.

1.4 Syllables (EXCLUDED)
------------------------
Paediatric phonological-awareness mode with word bands graded for
children. Adults sit at ceiling on counting and mapping levels, and
the construct (segmentation skill) has no adult deficit to measure.
Not in the battery. If a ceiling check is wanted for the thesis
appendix, run level 3 (beat-paced) on five pilot adults once:
expected accuracy above 95 percent and asynchrony SD under 60 ms at
the 500 ms beat; anything else is a bug report, not a finding.

1.5 Adaptive (EXCLUDED from the battery)
----------------------------------------
The controller ramps a healthy hand to bpm_max (180) and the config's
own headless measurement says the trailing hit rate only re-enters
the 65 to 80 band around trial 53, past the 40-trial block. A
healthy 40-trial block therefore measures the cap, not the
controller. The band-keeping claim stays on the headless simulation
already in the config commentary. Optional: run one dominant-hand
block at the end of visit 2 on participants who finish early, and
report bpm_max and the trailing hit rate descriptively.

1.6 Rhythm (both hands, one fixed track)
----------------------------------------
Primary: signed mean asynchrony per hand (time_difference_ms on hit
rows, notebook sec_rhythm), asynchrony SD, mean absolute offset
(block_summary.beat_offset_stats.beat_offset_abs_mean_ms), hit rate;
interval CV and the tempo-tracking r from sec_tap_variability.
Known effects to reproduce:
  Rh1 Negative mean asynchrony: taps precede the beat by tens of ms
      (Repp 2005, Psychonomic Bulletin and Review; Repp and Su
      2013). One-sample test per hand, mean below zero.
  Rh2 Asynchrony SD in the tens of ms for a comfortable tempo (Rose
      et al. 2019 report about 39 ms finger-tapping asynchrony,
      cited in docs/research/new_modes/movement-disorders.md).
  Rh3 No strong dominance prediction; report paired.
Reliability class: good for asynchrony SD and absolute offset,
moderate for the signed mean (bias is small relative to noise).
Claim limits: audio_offset_ms (40) is a configured constant, not a
measured latency on the study laptop; measure it once with a
microphone against the trigger event if the thesis wants absolute
asynchrony, otherwise report within-device numbers. The scoring
windows are game feel, not research criteria (rhythm.py).

1.7 Mirror (both hands)
-----------------------
Primary: mirror.mean_gap_ms (absolute right-left onset difference on
clean pairs), n_clean_pairs, hit rate, right_hand_mean_rt_ms and
left_hand_mean_rt_ms (a within-trial asymmetry).
Known effects to reproduce:
  M1 Homologous in-phase pressing is the stable coordination mode
     (Kelso 1984): mean gap well under the 350 ms gate, expected
     under about 60 ms. Descriptive with CI.
  M2 Dominant-hand press leads the non-dominant one: paired RT
     within the same trial, dominant faster (same basis as R2).
Reliability class: unknown for this device; predicted moderate.
Claim limits: the adaptive cadence engine underneath moves the BPM,
so the gap is measured across a changing pace; report bpm_min and
bpm_max alongside. Efficacy of bilateral training is contested and
not claimed (mirror.py).

1.8 Force Pilot (both hands, short form, level frozen at 1)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Primary (notebook sec_force_tracking, offline from raw.csv): RMSE
and MAE in percent of max, time in corridor, cross-correlation lag,
press versus release MAE, section MAE (hold_in, ramp_up, hold_top,
release, sine, assess_sos), Lodha sub-1 Hz spectral ratio. Block
level: force_pilot.overall.mae_pct, time_in_corridor,
release_mae_pct, press_mae_pct, section_mae_pct.
Known effects to reproduce:
  F1 Error grows with target bandwidth and unpredictability: MAE
     assess_sos > sine > holds, ordered within participant
     (Kurillo 2005 varied target shape for exactly this reason;
     Lodha 2013 puts the healthy variability structure below 1 Hz).
  F2 Visuomotor lag positive, of order 100 to 300 ms (a sanity
     check on the corridor timing, not a literature norm).
  F3 Release error approximately equal to press error in young
     adults. Release-specific impairment is the Parkinson's and
     ageing marker (Davidson 2026; Naik 2011, in force_pilot.py), so
     a young cohort should show a small difference; report the
     paired difference with its CI as an equivalence statement.
  F4 Time in corridor above the 0.8 promotion criterion on most
     runs: confirms level 1 is the right entry point and that the
     ladder would move for a healthy hand.
Error versus level (the effect the brief names) cannot come from a
one-run-per-finger battery with the ladder frozen. It comes from a
separate level pilot: six participants, one extra visit,
runs_per_finger 3 with the ladder free, so each finger reaches level
2 or 3 and MAE by level can be plotted. Cheap, and it belongs in the
instrumentation section.
Reliability class: moderate to good for hand-level MAE and time in
corridor (four runs per hand); per-finger numbers rest on a single
27 s run and are reported but not given an MDC.
Claim limits: the probe is a maximal flat-finger press on a pad, not
grip or pinch MVC; SingleTact accuracy and drift at low force is
uncharacterised (a bench check with known masses must precede the
first participant; force_pilot.py says so and the thesis owns it);
research numbers come from the offline re-score, never the in-game
score.

1.9 Lighthouse (both hands, short form, level frozen at 3)
----------------------------------------------------------
Primary (notebook sec_precision_hold, offline): lit CoV and MAE,
dark MAE, lit-dark delta (percent of max), post-fade drift rate and
direction, time in band; echo trials: constant, variable and
absolute reproduction error at 2 s and 10 s delay. Block level:
lighthouse.overall.lit_mae_pct, dark_mae_pct, lit_dark_delta_pct,
drift_rate_pct_s, echo.by_delay.
Known effects to reproduce:
  L1 Dark error above lit error (delta above zero, paired
     one-sided) but small: in Li et al. 2015 controls "barely moved"
     when feedback was removed while carpal tunnel patients
     collapsed (cited in lighthouse.py). A healthy delta that is
     positive and small is the validity result.
  L2 Reproduction error grows with delay: absolute error at 10 s
     above 2 s (delay is the memory load; standard force-sense
     paradigm, lighthouse.py).
  L3 Steadiness worse at lower targets: lit CoV correlates
     negatively with target percent across trials (steadiness
     deficits live at the low end, Camacho-Villa 2025 range 5 to 25
     percent MVC).
Reliability class: good for lit CoV and MAE (published pinch force
sense ICCs 0.6 to 0.9, Scientific Reports 2024 and BMC Musculoskelet
Disord 2020 in docs/research/new_modes/force-control.md); moderate
for the delta (a difference of two noisy numbers) and for echo error
at one trial per finger per delay.
Claim limits: flat-finger normal force, not thumb-finger pinch (the
CTS literature's grip); the bilateral echo is cross-hand by design
(one hand studies, the other reproduces), so echo error carries a
transfer component and is reported as cross-hand matching, not
same-finger reproduction.

1.10 Buzz Hunt (both hands, short form: localisation and span only)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary: loc.accuracy per hand and per lane, the confusion matrix
(adjacent-finger error share), catch false-alarm rate and d-prime,
threshold.<hand>.final_ms and estimate_ms with the floor flag, span
max_correct and novel versus Hebb accuracy.
Known effects to reproduce:
  B1 Localisation near ceiling: accuracy above 90 percent per hand.
  B2 Errors cluster on neighbouring fingers: adjacent share of
     errors above 0.5 (mislocalisations fall on nearer fingers,
     Schweizer, Maier, Braun and Birbaumer 2000, Somatosensory and
     Motor Research 17:309-316; Weber 2023 in buzz_hunt.py for the
     misreferral map).
  B3 Guessing priced: catch false alarms under 10 percent.
  B4 Tactile-only span around 4 items, well below visual span
     (buzz_hunt.py and echo.py both state the ceiling); Hebb trials
     at least as accurate as novel trials.
Reliability class: poor for accuracy (ceiling restricts range),
moderate for span; the duration threshold is expected to sit at the
40 ms floor for most healthy hands and is reported as a censored
proportion, not an ICC.
Claim limits: ERM rise and stop time about 20 ms is uncharacterised,
so thresholds are within-device only; the gap stage is dropped from
the battery because its 35 ms floor sits inside the healthy temporal
discrimination range (about 30 to 50 ms, Borngraber 2022 in
docs/research/new_modes/carpal-nerve.md) and would be censored; the
distractor stage is dropped for time. For carpal tunnel this suite
is measurement, not therapy (Jerosch-Herold 2016).

1.11 Echo (one bilateral 8-lane block)
--------------------------------------
Primary: span, total_correct, product_score (Kessels total: span x
correct sequences), n_omissions, error taxonomy from the notebook
(transposition, intrusion, omission), Hebb versus novel accuracy by
length.
Known effects to reproduce:
  E1 Cohort span in the region of the Corsi norm: healthy adults
     average 6.2 blocks, SD 1.3 (Kessels et al. 2000, Applied
     Neuropsychology 7:252-258, n = 70). Eight lanes with revisits,
     bimodal presentation and a press response are named
     deviations, so E1 is a plausibility band, not a norm
     comparison; a cohort median of 5 to 8 is consistent.
  E2 Hebb repetition learning: hidden-sequence trials more accurate
     than novel trials at matched length (Hebb 1961; Couture and
     Tremblay 2007, in echo.py).
  E3 Serial-order errors dominate: transpositions and omissions
     outnumber intrusions.
Reliability class: moderate (span is a coarse integer; the product
score has the better distribution, echo.py).
Claim limits: never read against Kessels or Farrell Pagulayan norms
as a test result; the bilateral block is a bimanual span and is
never pooled with unilateral echo blocks (none are run here).
Choosing the 8-lane block is deliberate: eight distinct locations is
the closest this rig gets to Corsi's nine.

1.12 Cross-mode summary
-----------------------
Strong for a healthy cohort (known effect, expected good
reliability): reaction, rhythm, lighthouse, force pilot, chords
(C1 to C4), echo. Group-level only: pattern (P1, P2 reliable at the
group level, individual score unreliable by published meta-analysis).
Ceiling or floor limited: buzz hunt (accuracy ceiling, threshold
floor), adaptive (bpm cap). Wrong population: syllables.


2. STUDY DESIGN
===============

2.1 Design and sample
---------------------
Within-subject, both hands, two visits. Every participant does the
same ten blocks at each visit. Hand is a within-subject factor
(dominant, non-dominant) mapped from the Edinburgh score; visit is
the retest factor.

N = 28 analysed; recruit 32 to absorb about 10 percent loss to
no-show at visit 2 or hardware failure.

ICC precision. Bonett (2002, Statistics in Medicine 21:1331-1335)
gives the sample size for a 95 percent CI of width w on an
intraclass correlation rho with k = 2 sessions as

  n = 1 + 8 z^2 (1 - rho)^2 (1 + rho)^2 / (2 w^2),  z = 1.96.

  rho 0.80, w 0.30 : n = 24
  rho 0.75, w 0.30 : n = 34
  rho 0.70, w 0.30 : n = 46
  rho 0.70, w 0.40 : n = 26
  rho 0.90, w 0.20 : n = 15

At n = 28 the expected CI width is about 0.27 at ICC 0.8, 0.38 at
0.7 and 0.48 at 0.6. So the study can place a good metric (0.8) in
the good band with a CI that stays above 0.5, and can tell a
moderate metric from a poor one; it cannot separate 0.70 from 0.75.
That is the honest precision statement for the thesis.

Paired power. Two-sided alpha 0.05, power 0.80: n = 28 detects a
paired effect of Cohen dz = 0.55; n = 24 detects dz = 0.60; n = 32
detects dz = 0.51. Dominance asymmetries in healthy young adults are
small to medium (tapping speed and consistency favour the dominant
hand, Peters 1980, Hubel 2013; force coordination often shows no
difference, Scientific Reports 2025), so the hand comparisons are
powered for medium effects only and are framed as validity checks
of direction. The primary outputs (normative ranges, ICCs, SEM,
MDC) do not depend on a significant asymmetry.

Retest interval: 7 days, accept 5 to 9. Same time of day within 2 h
(reaction time has a time-of-day component). Same rig, same laptop,
same config file (record the git commit hash and the config path in
the intake sheet; both are already in metadata.json via
config_snapshot and software_version).

2.2 Counterbalancing
--------------------
Two mode orders and two hand orders, four cells, assigned by code:

  code mod 4 = 1 : order A, dominant hand first   (P01, P05, ...)
  code mod 4 = 2 : order B, dominant hand first   (P02, P06, ...)
  code mod 4 = 3 : order A, non-dominant first    (P03, P07, ...)
  code mod 4 = 0 : order B, non-dominant first    (P04, P08, ...)

Seven per cell at n = 28 if every code from P01 to P28 completes;
with 32 recruited and no-shows falling where they fall, the cells
will be unequal by one or two, which costs nothing for the
within-subject outputs and is reported. A participant keeps their
cell at both visits, so visit 2 is a true replicate of visit 1 and
the ICC is not diluted by an order change.

  Order A: timing set, then force set, then pattern.
  Order B: force set, then timing set, then pattern.

  Timing set (fixed internal order): reaction hand 1, reaction hand
  2, mirror, rhythm, echo.
  Force set (fixed internal order): force pilot, lighthouse, chords,
  buzz hunt. Force pilot goes first inside the set so the max-press
  probe is taken once and reused by lighthouse (probe_max_age_s is
  21600 s, so a probe taken this visit is reused).

Pattern is always last because it is the longest single block, the
only single-hand block, and the one whose individual score is least
reliable (Section 1.2); if a visit runs over, pattern is the block
that is dropped and logged as missed. A full Latin square over ten
blocks is not feasible at n = 28 and would not buy anything the two
set orders do not already control (the fatigue gradient across the
visit).

------------------------------------------------

Reference full-form durations, for the thesis to state what was
shortened: reaction 25 trials about 3 min (config comment, M);
pattern 10 takes 12 to 25 min in patients (config, M) and about 11
min for a healthy adult (A); chords 100 chords about 8.5 min (A);
syllables 40 words 8.5 min (M, excluded); buzz hunt one hand 8.5 min
and both hands 16.8 min, capped at 15 (M); force pilot two runs per
finger both hands about 13 min (A); lighthouse two holds and one
echo per finger both hands about 11 min (A); echo ladder to span 6
about 12 trials, a few minutes (config, M); adaptive 40 trials 30 to
43 s in the recorded test blocks on disk (M, excluded).

Visit 1 booking is 65 minutes (15 minutes before the clock starts:
consent, Edinburgh short form, hand measurement, code assignment).
Visit 2 booking is 55 minutes.

The 50 minute figure has no slack. The run sheet's hard stop is at
50 minutes: whatever block is in progress finishes (every mode
keeps its data on an early end), the remainder is logged as missed
on the intake sheet, and the analysis treats it as missing for that
metric only.

therefore examples, and the distribution below is the measurement.

Simulated cohort (scripts/simulate_cohort.py, 2026-09-02): 28 codes
x 2 visits, all 560 blocks completed, each visit run the way the RA
runs it (login with the intake, STUDY BATTERY, every NEXT UP step,
end session), on the same model participant with a per-person
reaction time (mean 300 ms, SD 35 ms between people) and the rests
taken as above. Minutes per visit, login and calibration allowance
included: minimum 41.9, 5th percentile 42.7, median 43.9, 95th
percentile 45.4, maximum 45.9; visit 1 and visit 2 the same within
0.1 min at the median; no visit over 50. Per block (median, range):
reaction 2.8 (2.2 to 3.7), mirror 0.55 (0.5 to 0.7), rhythm 2.05,
echo 2.0 (1.1 to 3.0, shorter when the ladder ends at a low span),
force pilot 5.1 (4.8 to 5.2), lighthouse 5.7, chords 4.3 (4.2 to
4.4), buzz hunt 5.1 (4.9 to 5.3), pattern 5.9 (5.5 to 6.5).

Two things the measurement leaves out: the max-press probe (the
model's calibration carries a fresh max, so force pilot runs its
eight runs and no probe; a real visit adds about 1.5 min before the
force set) and the time a person spends reading a card before
pressing Start (the 10 s per transition is the design's allowance,
not a measurement). The model is also a lower bound on the human
parts of a visit: it never asks a question, never needs a rest
repeated and releases every self-paced rest 1.5 s after its floor.
With the probe added the simulated visits land at about 43.5 to
47.5 minutes, inside the budget with two and a half to six minutes
of slack for the parts a model cannot stand in for. Mirror at 0.6 min is 32 trials at the mode's
pace, well under the 1.5 min allowed; every other block came in at
or under its design figure except echo (3.2 against 3.0) and
pattern (6.4 against 6.0), both within half a minute.

2.4 Short forms and frozen ladders: exact config keys
-----------------------------------------------------
Implemented: the battery is a protocol preset in config/default.yaml
(protocol.presets.study_battery, id healthy_baseline_v1). The hub's
STUDY BATTERY button (or B) builds the plan for the logged-in code
(cell from the code, hands from the dominant hand entered at login)
and runs it through the engine's protocol runner, laying the keys
below over the live config for the length of the run and putting
them back when the last block ends or the session ends. Between
blocks the results screen shows as usual and NEXT UP carries the
next step, so every block keeps its GET READY countdown and its own
rests; the session strip reads BATTERY k/10. Each block's
metadata.json carries battery.id, battery.cell and battery.position,
and its config_snapshot shows the short-form keys it ran under.
No --config file and no per-participant yaml is needed; a study
laptop only needs session.suggest_code: always so the login offers
P01 on an empty tree (the default, auto, offers a code once one
exists on the machine).

The keys the preset applies, with the values the design fixed
(tests/test_study_battery.py pins this list exactly: nothing outside
it is touched, so a scoring, window or detection key can never creep
in through the preset):

  reaction:
    sub_mode: choice
    block_trials: 25
    response_windows_s: [2.0]     # one rung: window never moves
  force_pilot:
    runs_per_finger: 1
    rest_s: 5.0
    level: 1
    promote_frac: 1.01            # never promotes
    demote_frac: 0.0              # never demotes
  lighthouse:
    holds_per_finger: 1
    echoes_per_finger: 1
    echo_delays_s: [2, 10]
    rest_s: 4.0
    level: 3                      # two dark windows, 45 percent
    promote_lit_mae_pct: -1       # never promotes
    promote_delta_pct: -1
    demote_delta_pct: 1000        # never demotes
  chords:
    subblocks: 3                  # within, within, cross
    trials_per_subblock: 20
    sync_windows_ms: [150]        # one rung: W fixed at 150 ms
  buzz_hunt:
    loc_trials_per_hand: 16
    distractor_trials_per_hand: 0
    span_trials: 8
    gap_trials_per_hand: 0
    catch_rate: 0.1
  pattern:
    short_session: true
    soc_cycles_per_block: 3
    random_block_trials: 32
  echo: defaults (ladder from 2, two per length, ceiling 9)
  rhythm: difficulty medium; the preset pins the track
    (Easy_Lemon.mp3, 126 s) and the battery opens the block on it
    without the song screen; if the file is missing the song screen
    opens and the RA picks it by hand.
  mirror: defaults.

Everything else runs on the shipped config/default.yaml as it is,
with config/user_settings.yaml left at its defaults on the study
laptop: the cue mix (buzz and sound before the target, nothing
after, target shown), test mode off, balanced targets, reports on,
EEG off, quick calibration on, ports auto-assigned in plug order
(RIGHT board first). The preset does not write these; the run sheet
checks them and every block's config_snapshot records them. The
intake fields the software does not hold (affected side, impairment
score) stay empty for a study code; the cohort is identified by the
code prefix and by battery.id healthy_baseline_v1 in every battery
block's metadata, and "healthy control" is written on the intake
sheet, not into metadata.json.

Ladder freezes were checked against the code: force_pilot promotes
when the last two runs are at or above promote_frac and demotes when
time in corridor is below demote_frac; lighthouse promotes when the
delta is at or under promote_delta_pct and demotes at or over
demote_delta_pct; chords and reaction take the window ladder as a
list and clamp the level to its length, so a one-entry list is a
fixed window. Each of these needs a regression test through the
real engine before visit 1 (Section 4.6).

Statistical cost of each short form:

  Force Pilot, one run per finger. Per-finger metrics rest on a
  single 27 s run, so per-finger ICCs are not estimable with useful
  precision and get no MDC; the hand-level mean over four runs is
  the unit. Error versus level is not measurable (level pilot,
  Section 1.8).

  Lighthouse, one hold and one echo per finger, two delays. The
  lit-dark delta per finger comes off one hold; the delay slope is a
  two-point contrast; hand-level pooling over four fingers is the
  unit. Lit CoV per finger still comes off about 8.8 s of lit hold
  (16 s x 55 percent lit at level 3), about 1760 samples at 200 Hz,
  which is enough for a steadiness estimate.

  Chords, 60 chords. About 20 within-hand chords per hand per visit
  across 11 chord types, so per-chord cells are cohort-pooled (about
  28 x 20 per hand); the start and end enslaving matrices are
  sub-block 1 and sub-block 2, so the start-to-end fatigue read is
  weak; cross-hand mirror versus non-mirror has about 10 chords a
  side.

  Buzz Hunt, 16 localisation trials per hand, 8 span trials, no gap
  or distractor stage. The duration staircase may close with fewer
  than the six reversals the estimate needs (estimate_ms then reads
  None); the notebook reports final_ms and the floor flag. Four
  localisation trials per finger per visit, so the confusion matrix
  is cohort-pooled.

  Pattern, 36-press takes and a 32-trial random take. The first
  cycle of every take is trimmed by the mode's RT hygiene, leaving
  24 analysable presses per take against 48 in the full form, so
  the probe-minus-flanker standard error grows by about 1.4;
  sequence exposure before the first probe is 9 cycles (108 trials)
  against 15 (180), so the expected rebound is smaller. The
  group-level test has 28 participants x 2 probes x 2 visits.

  Reaction, window fixed at 2.0 s. No cost: a healthy adult almost
  never exceeds 1.2 s, and one window across hands and visits is a
  benefit.

4.3 Descriptives and normative ranges
-------------------------------------
Per metric and hand_role, visit 1: n, mean, SD, median, IQR, 5th
and 95th percentiles. Visit 2 the same, kept separate. The visit 1
table is the normative table the thesis prints. Distribution plots
per metric, with the known-effect reference (for example Kessels
6.2 for span) drawn as a line where one exists.

4.4 Dominant versus non-dominant
--------------------------------
Per metric with a hand_role pair, visit 1 (visit 2 as replication):
paired difference (dominant minus non-dominant), 95 percent CI,
paired t, Cohen dz = mean difference / SD of differences with a
bootstrap CI (10000 resamples, seeded, the notebook already has
BOOT_N and BOOT_SEED), Shapiro-Wilk on the differences; where the
differences are non-normal or n under 20 for a metric, the Wilcoxon
signed-rank test with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation
as the effect size. Report both when they disagree. Directional
hypotheses from Section 1 (R2, M2) are one-sided; everything else
two-sided with no claim beyond the CI.

4.5 Test-retest, SEM, MDC and practice
--------------------------------------
Per metric and hand_role, participants with both visits:
  ICC(2,1) via icc_two_one (two-way random, absolute agreement,
  single measure; Shrout and Fleiss 1979 form 2,1), with the exact
  F-based 95 percent CI (McGraw and Wong 1996 formulae, as
  implemented in pingouin or coded in Setup and tested against a
  known example).
  ICC(3,1) alongside (consistency): the gap between the two shows
  how much of the disagreement is a uniform shift (practice).
  SEM = SD_pooled x sqrt(1 - ICC), SD_pooled over both visits.
  MDC95 = 1.96 x sqrt(2) x SEM (Weir 2005, J Strength Cond Res
  19:231-240). Report MDC also as a fraction of the visit 1 SD so a
  reader sees whether the device can detect a change smaller than
  the normal spread.
  Bland-Altman: bias (visit 2 minus visit 1) with 95 percent CI and
  limits of agreement, per metric.
  Practice effect: the Bland-Altman bias is the practice effect;
  paired dz and CI on it. Expected: none for reaction (R3), a
  positive shift for pattern (P2, by design), small gains elsewhere.
  Metrics flagged in Section 1 as poor by construction (pattern
  learning score, buzz hunt accuracy at ceiling, thresholds at
  floor) get their ICC printed with the reason and no MDC.

4.6 Known-effect validity checks (pre-specified)
------------------------------------------------
One table, one row per check, from Section 1: R1 R2 R3, P1 P2 P3,
C1 C2 C3 C4, Rh1 Rh2, M1 M2, F1 F2 F3 F4, L1 L2 L3, B1 B2 B3 B4,
E1 E2 E3. Each row: statistic, CI, test, effect size, pass or fail
against the stated criterion. This table is the known-effect check
of the thesis, decided before the data comes in; it is construct
evidence (the game reproduces what the literature predicts), not
criterion validity against a clinical measure, which a healthy
cohort cannot give. No other inferential test is run; everything
else is descriptive with CIs.

5. ETHICS AND RUN SHEET
=======================

5.1 What the ethics application must cover
------------------------------------------
Risk level: low risk under the NHMRC National Statement (2023
update; "low risk" is research where the only foreseeable risk is
one of discomfort; NHMRC, National Statement on Ethical Conduct in
Human Research, Section 2.1). Curtin HREC low-risk pathway.

Participants: adults 18 and over from the psychology participant
pool, credit per the pool's standard rate; students of the
supervisor or the student researcher are excluded to avoid a
dependent relationship. Inclusion: normal or corrected vision,
able to sit at a laptop for 50 minutes. Exclusion: current hand,
wrist or forearm injury or pain; diagnosed neurological or
musculoskeletal condition affecting the hands; skin condition or
open wound on the hands; known sensitivity to vibration.

Procedure, in plain words: two visits of about an hour, one week
apart; hands rest flat on a 3D printed frame with a small force pad
under each fingertip; short games on a laptop screen; light presses
only; small vibration motors buzz a fingertip for a quarter of a
second as a cue; a ruler measurement of each hand; four questions on
hand preference; age and sex.

Foreseeable discomforts and their controls:
  Vibration: eccentric-rotating-mass motors at fixed drive, 250 ms
    bursts, felt as a phone-style buzz. No heat, no electrical
    contact with the skin (the motor is inside the frame). Anyone
    who finds it unpleasant can stop; buzz_before can be turned off
    for that participant and the change is logged in cue_flags and
    the intake sheet.
  Sustained light pressing: targets are at most 40 percent of the
    participant's own comfortable maximum press, holds are 16 s,
    runs under 30 s, with rests built in and every rest skippable
    only by the participant's choice. Mild finger fatigue is the
    expected worst case.
  Skin contact: pads and frame wiped with 70 percent isopropyl
    between participants; participants wash or sanitise hands on
    arrival; disposable finger cots offered.
  Electrical safety: the boards run at 5 V from the laptop USB
    port; no mains-powered part touches the participant.
  Screen and sound: 60 Hz display with no flashing patterns; laptop
    speaker at a moderate fixed volume set once and recorded.
  Boredom or frustration: games are short and scored; participants
    may skip a block or withdraw at any time without loss of credit.

Data: participant code only in every file the software writes;
age, sex, handedness and hand size on the intake sheet keyed by
code; the consent log is the only link between name and code and
is destroyed at study close. Recorded data are trial tables, 200 Hz
force traces and configuration snapshots; none is identifying.
Storage on Curtin-approved storage, not personal cloud folders;
retention per Curtin's research data policy; the software
repository is public on GitHub and holds no participant data
(sessions/ is gitignored).
Participants may withdraw their data until analysis begins.

Also state: no deception beyond the standard "some games repeat a
hidden pattern you are not told about" (pattern, buzz hunt, echo),
disclosed in the debrief; no clinical assessment or feedback is
given; the device is a research prototype, not a medical device.
