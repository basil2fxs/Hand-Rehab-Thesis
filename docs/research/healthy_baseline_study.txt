Why this study exists. No patients are available this semester. The
thesis still needs a results chapter with real hands on the device,
so the rig is turned into a measurement instrument and characterised
on healthy adults: what does each game measure in a normal hand, how
repeatable is it inside one sitting, does it move when the same
person plays the same task an hour later, and does it reproduce the
effects the literature says a healthy hand shows. That is a
normative and psychometric study, not a treatment study, and it is a
legitimate thesis result on its own: a device whose numbers are
stable and reproduce known effects is the precondition for any later
patient work, and a device whose numbers are not stable tells the
next student what to fix.

What this study cannot show, and the thesis must say so in the same
breath. A healthy cohort says nothing about sensitivity to
impairment (whether a stroke or carpal tunnel hand scores
differently), nothing about responsiveness (whether the numbers move
with recovery), nothing about the 65 to 80 percent challenge band or
any other therapy claim, and nothing about clinical validity against
an accepted assessment. The normative table is a description of 28
young, mostly right-handed university students on this rig, not a
population norm, and it is read against literature values as an
order-of-magnitude plausibility check only. Reliability measured in
a stable healthy hand is an upper bound on what a changing patient
hand would show, and a within-session reliability is an upper bound
again on a between-day one, because everything that drifts overnight
(sleep, time of day, how the hands are placed on the pads) is held
still. Most importantly, a change measured from the start of a
sitting to the end of it is a change in PERFORMANCE, not a
demonstration of learning: conditions that raise end-of-practice
scores can leave a delayed retention test untouched or worse, and
the delayed test is the better indicator of learning (Kantak and
Winstein 2012, Behavioural Brain Research). The thesis has to say
that in the same sentence as the pre-post result. The known-effect
checks in Section 4.6 are
construct checks: the game reproduces an effect the literature
predicts, which is evidence the measurement is of the right thing,
not evidence the device is a valid clinical instrument.


DECISIONS IN ONE SCREEN
=======================

- Within-subject, both hands, N = 28 analysed (recruit 32), ONE
  sitting per person. Booking 105 minutes: 15 minutes of consent and
  intake off the clock, then a PLAY ALL run with a design target of
  85 minutes and a hard stop at 90.
- Twenty blocks in three passes. PRE: the nine short-form blocks.
  MID: adaptive and syllables once each, so all ten modes are
  played. POST: the same nine blocks, same order, same hands, about
  40 minutes of other games later. The phase word (pre, mid, post)
  is written into every block's metadata and every trial row.
- All ten modes are played. Adaptive and syllables are played ONCE
  and reported descriptively: neither has a healthy-adult effect
  worth measuring twice (Sections 1.4 and 1.5), and together they
  cost about 2.5 minutes.
- Two scheduled 3 minute rests: one after the PRE pass, one at the
  POST pass's set boundary. The card counts the rest down and holds
  the Start button for the first 60 seconds; the rest actually taken
  is logged. One 60 second stretch at the PRE set boundary, which
  never holds the button.
- Two mode orders (A, B) crossed with two hand orders, assigned by
  participant code. The POST pass runs in the same order as the PRE
  pass within a person, so every mode has the same pre-to-post gap.
- Pattern runs on the dominant hand only, last in each pass, in its
  short form. Echo runs one bilateral 8-lane block per pass. Every
  other mode runs both hands.
- Every difficulty ladder that would otherwise move mid-block is
  frozen by config, so the PRE block and the POST block are the same
  task and the difference between them is the person.
- Participants are P01 to P32. The code goes in the login NAME field
  (the login suggests the next free code off the sessions tree, so
  it is never mistyped) and keys the sessions tree, the index, the
  notebook and the hidden sequences. Names live only in the consent
  log.
- What the participant sees: after a POST block, the mode just
  played against that person's own first go today, in the mode's own
  words ("22 ms faster than your first go"), plus a short chip for
  every other mode with two goes behind it. At PLAY ALL DONE the
  NEXT UP card becomes a TODAY table: one row per mode, first to
  latest, change, green for better, grey for "about the same", amber
  for worse. Nothing on it mentions anybody else.
- Primary outputs: normative table (median, IQR, 5th to 95th), paired
  dominant versus non-dominant with Cohen dz, within-session
  reliability (split-half inside a block, ICC(3,1) and ICC(2,1)
  between the PRE and POST blocks) with SEM and MDC95 labelled as
  within-session, the PRE to POST change with dz and a responder
  count, and one pre-specified known-effect check per mode plus the
  pre-registered learning checks. One tidy CSV.
- Reaction is the stability anchor. It is the one mode expected NOT
  to move, so what it does read is fatigue and drift across the
  sitting.


1. WHAT A HEALTHY COHORT CAN PROVE, PER MODE
============================================

Format per mode: primary metric(s) and where they come from; the
known healthy-adult effect the game must reproduce; expected
test-retest reliability class; honest claim limits. "block_stats"
means the mode's own block_stats() dict, folded into
metadata.json under block_summary.<mode>; "notebook" means the
offline re-score in analysis/session_analysis.ipynb.

Reliability classes use Koo and Li (2016): below 0.5 poor, 0.5 to
0.75 moderate, 0.75 to 0.9 good, above 0.9 excellent, and are
predictions to be tested, not promises.

1.1 Reaction (choice, one hand per block)
-----------------------------------------
Primary: median_rt_ms, lapse_like_rate, n_false_start_total /
n_attempts, accuracy (block_stats.reaction). Secondary: sd_rt_ms,
p10_rt_ms, slope_rt_ms_per_trial, spearman_rho_rt_vs_fp.
Known effects to reproduce:
  R1 The exponential foreperiod works: cohort median of the
     Spearman rho between RT and foreperiod near zero (|rho| under
     0.2) and false starts under 10 percent of attempts. This is the
     anticipation control the mode was built for (Niemi and Naatanen
     1981; Naatanen 1971, both cited in reaction.py).
  R2 Dominant hand faster than non-dominant. The dominant advantage
     is well replicated for tapping speed and consistency (Peters
     1980; Hubel et al. 2013) and is small for reaction time, so R2
     is a directional check, not a powered primary outcome.
  R3 No practice effect on median RT across visits. The PVT family
     the block is modelled on shows no change in median RT or lapses
     over 16 repeated administrations (Basner et al. 2017, Sleep).
Reliability class: good to excellent for median RT (the PVT
tradition; the block is 25 scorable trials, near the validated 3 min
PVT-B of Basner, Mollicone and Dinges 2011).
Claim limits: absolute RTs are device-specific (60 Hz display, force
threshold clock, unmeasured panel latency; Woods 2015 and Plant and
Turner 2009 in reaction.py) and measured to an audio-tactile-visual
cue under the shipped cue defaults. Report them as this device's
numbers, never against published button-press norms. Simple RT is
not run (time), so the choice-versus-simple contrast is not
available.

1.2 Pattern, SRTT (dominant hand only, short form)
--------------------------------------------------
Primary: session_learning_score_ms (probe minus flanking trained
takes, block_stats.pattern.probe_scores) and accuracy_rebound_pct.
Secondary: per-take mean RT curve, random-take baseline RT.
Known effects to reproduce:
  P1 Positive learning score in the PRE block (cohort mean above
     zero).
     The probe rebound is one of the most replicated effects in
     cognitive psychology (Nissen and Bullemer 1987: 327 to 163 ms;
     Kal et al. 2016 meta-analysis: 69 ms mean rebound in healthy
     and less-affected hands, cited in pattern.py). With the short
     form's smaller exposure expect tens of ms, not 69.
  P2 Larger learning score in the POST block than the PRE block,
     and a trained-take RT below the PRE block's. The trained
     sequence is seeded from the participant code, so the POST
     block re-presents the same material after about 40 minutes of
     other games: a WITHIN-session retention probe after a filled
     delay. It is not the overnight test the two-visit design had,
     and the thesis says so: end-of-practice performance and
     learning that survives a night are different things (Kantak
     and Winstein 2012).
  P3 Accuracy drops on probe takes (rebound above zero).
Reliability class: POOR for the individual learning score, and this
must be said in the thesis before the data comes in. The 2023
meta-analysis of SRTT test-retest correlations pooled r = 0.28 (95
percent CI 0.16 to 0.40; Oliveira, Hayiou-Thomas and Henderson
2023, Royal Society Open Science), with no moderator rescuing it.
The group-level effect is reliable; the individual score is not a
trait. Report the ICC, expect it to be low, and do not compute an
MDC for it.
Claim limits: dominant hand only, so no asymmetry. Single-session
rebounds can be temporary adaptation (Trofimova 2020 in
pattern.py); P2 is the memory claim. Never tell participants a
sequence exists (Boyd and Winstein, in pattern.py).
Riff files: a sequence file loaded on the study laptop
(config/pattern_sequence.yaml, Settings > Riff file) REPLACES the
built-in ten-take layout and the name-seeded riff for every pattern
block. Everything above assumes the built-in material, so either run
the cohort with no file loaded, or load the SAME file for every
participant and leave it alone for the whole study. The file's
schedule id (the sha256 of its bytes) lands in every block's
metadata, so a mid-study swap is recoverable in the analysis, but it
splits the cohort into two groups that cannot be pooled. A file with
explicit: true tells the participant the riff, which is a different
design from this one and is excluded from the implicit measure.

1.3 Chords (both hands, short form)
-----------------------------------
Primary: median_er (enslaving ratio, quiet-finger leak over target
press), median_span_ms (chord togetherness), clean hit rate
(chord_outcome_classes.hit / all), per-hand versions in per_hand;
cross-hand: hit_rate_mirror, hit_rate_nonmirror, median_lag_ms,
bilateral_deficit.
Known effects to reproduce:
  C1 Cohort median ER under 0.15. Healthy hands leak roughly 5 to 15
     percent of instructed force at light effort (Abolins et al.
     2020, cited in chords.py: 8 to 10 percent at about 25 percent
     MVC).
  C2 The computed difficulty rank D predicts outcome: negative
     Spearman correlation between D and per-chord hit rate, positive
     between D and per-chord median ER, on the cohort-pooled
     per-chord table. This tests the adjacency rule (enslaving
     largest between neighbours, Zatsiorsky, Li and Latash 2000).
  C3 Ring finger the most enslaved: in the cohort-mean
     chord-conditioned matrix the ring column carries the largest
     leak (Hager-Ross and Schieber 2000; Chiang 2004, in chords.py).
  C4 Cross-hand mirror chords hit more often and land tighter than
     non-mirror chords (the symmetry advantage: Kelso 1984; Haken,
     Kelso and Bunz 1985; Mechsner 2001, in chords.py).
  C5 Dominant versus non-dominant ER: no strong prediction. Force
     coordination studies in young adults often find no dominance
     difference (Scientific Reports 2025, "Comparable neural and
     behavioural performance in dominant and non-dominant hands
     during grasping tasks"), so report the paired result with its
     CI and treat a null as expected.
Reliability class: moderate for ER (a ratio of two noisy peaks
normalised by that day's calibration), good for span_ms.
Claim limits: ER and the matrices are device- and posture-specific
and chord-conditioned (an upper bound on the single-finger cell);
literature values are order-of-magnitude context only. The short
form gives about 20 within-hand chords per hand per visit, so
per-chord cells are cohort-pooled, never per person.

1.4 Syllables (EXCLUDED)
------------------------
Paediatric phonological-awareness mode with word bands graded for
children. Adults sit at ceiling on counting and mapping levels, and
the construct (segmentation skill) has no adult deficit to measure.
Not in the battery. If a ceiling check is wanted for the thesis
appendix, run level 3 (beat-paced) on five pilot adults once:
expected accuracy above 95 percent and asynchrony SD under 60 ms at
the 500 ms beat; anything else is a bug report, not a finding.

1.5 Adaptive (EXCLUDED from the battery)
----------------------------------------
The controller ramps a healthy hand to bpm_max (180) and the config's
own headless measurement says the trailing hit rate only re-enters
the 65 to 80 band around trial 53, past the 40-trial block. A
healthy 40-trial block therefore measures the cap, not the
controller. The band-keeping claim stays on the headless simulation
already in the config commentary. In the single-session design
adaptive IS played, once, in the middle pass on the dominant hand,
because playing every mode was the point of the change; bpm_max and
the trailing hit rate are reported descriptively and adaptive
appears in no reliability or pre-post table.

1.6 Rhythm (both hands, one fixed track)
----------------------------------------
Primary: signed mean asynchrony per hand (time_difference_ms on hit
rows, notebook sec_rhythm), asynchrony SD, mean absolute offset
(block_summary.beat_offset_stats.beat_offset_abs_mean_ms), hit rate;
interval CV and the tempo-tracking r from sec_tap_variability.
Known effects to reproduce:
  Rh1 Negative mean asynchrony: taps precede the beat by tens of ms
      (Repp 2005, Psychonomic Bulletin and Review; Repp and Su
      2013). One-sample test per hand, mean below zero.
  Rh2 Asynchrony SD in the tens of ms for a comfortable tempo (Rose
      et al. 2019 report about 39 ms finger-tapping asynchrony,
      cited in docs/research/new_modes/movement-disorders.md).
  Rh3 No strong dominance prediction; report paired.
Reliability class: good for asynchrony SD and absolute offset,
moderate for the signed mean (bias is small relative to noise).
Claim limits: audio_offset_ms (40) is a configured constant, not a
measured latency on the study laptop; measure it once with a
microphone against the trigger event if the thesis wants absolute
asynchrony, otherwise report within-device numbers. The scoring
windows are game feel, not research criteria (rhythm.py).

1.7 Mirror (both hands)
-----------------------
Primary: mirror.mean_gap_ms (absolute right-left onset difference on
clean pairs), n_clean_pairs, hit rate, right_hand_mean_rt_ms and
left_hand_mean_rt_ms (a within-trial asymmetry).
Known effects to reproduce:
  M1 Homologous in-phase pressing is the stable coordination mode
     (Kelso 1984): mean gap well under the 350 ms gate, expected
     under about 60 ms. Descriptive with CI.
  M2 Dominant-hand press leads the non-dominant one: paired RT
     within the same trial, dominant faster (same basis as R2).
Reliability class: unknown for this device; predicted moderate.
Claim limits: the adaptive cadence engine underneath moves the BPM,
so the gap is measured across a changing pace; report bpm_min and
bpm_max alongside. Efficacy of bilateral training is contested and
not claimed (mirror.py).

1.8 Force Pilot (both hands, short form, level frozen at 1)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Primary (notebook sec_force_tracking, offline from raw.csv): RMSE
and MAE in percent of max, time in corridor, cross-correlation lag,
press versus release MAE, section MAE (hold_in, ramp_up, hold_top,
release, sine, assess_sos), Lodha sub-1 Hz spectral ratio. Block
level: force_pilot.overall.mae_pct, time_in_corridor,
release_mae_pct, press_mae_pct, section_mae_pct.
Known effects to reproduce:
  F1 Error grows with target bandwidth and unpredictability: MAE
     assess_sos > sine > holds, ordered within participant
     (Kurillo 2005 varied target shape for exactly this reason;
     Lodha 2013 puts the healthy variability structure below 1 Hz).
  F2 Visuomotor lag positive, of order 100 to 300 ms (a sanity
     check on the corridor timing, not a literature norm).
  F3 Release error approximately equal to press error in young
     adults. Release-specific impairment is the Parkinson's and
     ageing marker (Davidson 2026; Naik 2011, in force_pilot.py), so
     a young cohort should show a small difference; report the
     paired difference with its CI as an equivalence statement.
  F4 Time in corridor above the 0.8 promotion criterion on most
     runs: confirms level 1 is the right entry point and that the
     ladder would move for a healthy hand.
Error versus level (the effect the brief names) cannot come from a
one-run-per-finger battery with the ladder frozen. It comes from a
separate level pilot: six participants, one extra visit,
runs_per_finger 3 with the ladder free, so each finger reaches level
2 or 3 and MAE by level can be plotted. Cheap, and it belongs in the
instrumentation section.
Reliability class: moderate to good for hand-level MAE and time in
corridor (four runs per hand); per-finger numbers rest on a single
27 s run and are reported but not given an MDC.
Claim limits: the probe is a maximal flat-finger press on a pad, not
grip or pinch MVC; SingleTact accuracy and drift at low force is
uncharacterised (a bench check with known masses must precede the
first participant; force_pilot.py says so and the thesis owns it);
research numbers come from the offline re-score, never the in-game
score.

1.10 Buzz Hunt (both hands, short form: localisation and span only)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary: loc.accuracy per hand and per lane at the fixed 150 ms
pulse, the confusion matrix (adjacent-finger error share), catch
false-alarm rate and d-prime, median localisation RT, the top
response-window level reached per hand (window.<hand>.top_level,
levels 3.0, 2.0, 1.5, 1.2 s), span max_correct and novel versus
Hebb accuracy.
2026-09 change, and why the threshold is gone: the localisation
pulse used to run a duration staircase and every correct answer
shortened the buzz until it could not be felt; the 40 ms floor was
the host's, not a perceptual one, and a coin ERM has barely started
moving when a 40 ms command ends (lag about 40 ms, rise about 87 ms,
Precision Microdrives 310-103 class values in config latency.*). The
pulse is now fixed at 150 ms (one firmware hold, inside the 50 to
200 ms band Kaaresoja and Linjama 2005 found usable on a phone
motor) and difficulty moves the response window instead: up a level
after 6 correct of the last 8 trials, down after 2 misses in the
last 4, level stamped on every row. threshold.* in block_summary is
empty unless buzz_hunt.duration_staircase was on for that block.
Known effects to reproduce:
  B1 Localisation near ceiling: accuracy above 90 percent per hand
     at the 150 ms pulse.
  B2 Errors cluster on neighbouring fingers: adjacent share of
     errors above 0.5 (mislocalisations fall on nearer fingers,
     Schweizer, Maier, Braun and Birbaumer 2000, Somatosensory and
     Motor Research 17:309-316; Weber 2023 in buzz_hunt.py for the
     misreferral map).
  B3 Guessing priced: catch false alarms under 10 percent.
  B4 Tactile-only span around 4 items, well below visual span
     (buzz_hunt.py and echo.py both state the ceiling); Hebb trials
     at least as accurate as novel trials.
  B5 The window ladder climbs: a healthy hand plays at level 1 (the
     2.0 s window) or higher inside its 16 trials, and usually
     reaches level 2 (a 92 percent responder reaches the top in
     about 22 trials on the headless simulation, so 16 trials allow
     two promotions; top_level counts the highest level a trial
     was played at, final_level where the ladder ended).
Reliability class: poor for accuracy (ceiling restricts range),
moderate for span; the top window level is an ordinal with four
values and 16 trials per hand, so it is reported as a distribution
and a proportion at each level, not an ICC. Median RT at the fixed
pulse is the continuous localisation number and gets the ICC.
Claim limits: ERM lag, rise and stop time are datasheet class
values until scripts/latency_check.py has been run on this rig, so
the gap threshold (not in the battery) is within-device only; the
gap stage is dropped from the battery because at its 120 ms floor
(the motor's spin-down) it cannot reach the healthy temporal
discrimination range (about 30 to 50 ms, Borngraber 2022 in
docs/research/new_modes/carpal-nerve.md); the distractor stage is
dropped for time. For carpal tunnel this suite is measurement, not
therapy (Jerosch-Herold 2016).

1.11 Echo (one bilateral 8-lane block)
--------------------------------------
Primary: span, total_correct, product_score (Kessels total: span x
correct sequences), n_omissions, error taxonomy from the notebook
(transposition, intrusion, omission), Hebb versus novel accuracy by
length.
Known effects to reproduce:
  E1 Cohort span in the region of the Corsi norm: healthy adults
     average 6.2 blocks, SD 1.3 (Kessels et al. 2000, Applied
     Neuropsychology 7:252-258, n = 70). Eight lanes with revisits,
     bimodal presentation and a press response are named
     deviations, so E1 is a plausibility band, not a norm
     comparison; a cohort median of 5 to 8 is consistent.
  E2 Hebb repetition learning: hidden-sequence trials more accurate
     than novel trials at matched length (Hebb 1961; Couture and
     Tremblay 2007, in echo.py).
  E3 Serial-order errors dominate: transpositions and omissions
     outnumber intrusions.
Reliability class: moderate (span is a coarse integer; the product
score has the better distribution, echo.py).
Claim limits: never read against Kessels or Farrell Pagulayan norms
as a test result; the bilateral block is a bimanual span and is
never pooled with unilateral echo blocks (none are run here).
Choosing the 8-lane block is deliberate: eight distinct locations is
the closest this rig gets to Corsi's nine.

1.12 Cross-mode summary
-----------------------
Strong for a healthy cohort (known effect, expected good
reliability): reaction, rhythm, force pilot, chords
(C1 to C4), echo. Group-level only: pattern (P1, P2 reliable at the
group level, individual score unreliable by published meta-analysis).
Ceiling or floor limited: buzz hunt (accuracy ceiling; the window
ladder tops out at level 3), adaptive (bpm cap). Wrong population:
syllables.


2. STUDY DESIGN
===============

2.1 Design and sample
---------------------
Within-subject, both hands, ONE session. Every participant plays the
same nine blocks early in the sitting (phase pre), two more modes in
the middle (phase mid) and the same nine blocks late (phase post).
Hand is a within-subject factor (dominant, non-dominant) taken from
the main hand picked at login, with the Edinburgh short form on the
paper intake sheet as the check. Phase is the repeat factor that the
two visits used to supply.

Why one session. Two visits cost two bookings, lose people between
them, and answer a question (is the number the same next week) that
a single semester of healthy volunteers does not need answered
first. One long sitting answers the question the thesis actually
wants: does a person's number move while they play, can the device
see the move, and can the person see it too. It also removes the
visit-2 no-show, which was the whole reason for recruiting 32 to
analyse 28.

N = 28 analysed; recruit 32. With one visit, 32 recruits should
yield 30 or more complete sessions, so the cells stay close to
even.

Paired power. Two-sided alpha 0.05, power 0.80: n = 28 detects a
paired effect of Cohen dz = 0.55; n = 24 detects dz = 0.60; n = 32
detects dz = 0.51. Both of the paired comparisons in this design
(dominant against non-dominant, and pre against post) are therefore
powered for medium effects only, and both are framed as checks of
DIRECTION with an effect size and a CI, not as hypothesis tests to
be won. Dominance asymmetries in healthy young adults are small to
medium (tapping speed and consistency favour the dominant hand,
Peters 1980, Hubel 2013; force coordination often shows no
difference, Scientific Reports 2025).

Reliability precision. The two-visit design used Bonett (2002,
Statistics in Medicine 21:1331-1335) for the width of an ICC
interval with k = 2 sessions:

  n = 1 + 8 z^2 (1 - rho)^2 (1 + rho)^2 / (2 w^2),  z = 1.96.

  rho 0.80, w 0.30 : n = 24
  rho 0.75, w 0.30 : n = 34
  rho 0.70, w 0.30 : n = 46
  rho 0.70, w 0.40 : n = 26
  rho 0.90, w 0.20 : n = 15

That arithmetic is unchanged: k = 2 is now the PRE block and the
POST block of one sitting rather than two visits. At n = 28 the
expected CI width is about 0.27 at ICC 0.8, 0.38 at 0.7 and 0.48 at
0.6, so the study can place a good metric in the good band with an
interval that stays above 0.5, and can tell a moderate metric from a
poor one; it cannot separate 0.70 from 0.75. What has changed is
what the number MEANS: a within-session ICC holds constant
everything a between-day ICC does not (the same seating, the same
calibration, the same hour, the same fatigue trajectory), so it is
an upper bound on between-day reliability and must be labelled as
such everywhere it is printed (Section 4.5).

Same rig, same laptop, same config file: record the git commit hash
and the config path on the intake sheet. Both are already in
metadata.json via config_snapshot and software_version.

2.2 Counterbalancing and the pass structure
-------------------------------------------
Two mode orders and two hand orders, four cells, assigned by code:

  code mod 4 = 1 : order A, dominant hand first   (P01, P05, ...)
  code mod 4 = 2 : order B, dominant hand first   (P02, P06, ...)
  code mod 4 = 3 : order A, non-dominant first    (P03, P07, ...)
  code mod 4 = 0 : order B, non-dominant first    (P04, P08, ...)

Seven per cell at n = 28. The cell is written into every block's
metadata (battery.cell). Unequal cells cost nothing for the
within-subject outputs and are reported.

  Order A: timing set, then force set, then pattern.
  Order B: force set, then timing set, then pattern.

  Timing set (fixed internal order): reaction hand 1, reaction hand
  2, mirror, rhythm, echo.
  Force set (fixed internal order): force pilot, chords, buzz hunt.

Pattern is last in each pass because it is the longest single block,
the only single-hand block, and the one whose individual score is
least reliable (Section 1.2); if the session runs over, the POST
pattern block is the one that is dropped and logged as missed.

The POST pass runs in the SAME order as the PRE pass for that
person. That is the point of the design: it makes the gap between a
mode's two goes about 40 minutes for every mode and every
participant, so "played early" and "played late" mean the same thing
in every row of the table. What it does not control is position
within the session, which is why the between-cell contrast matters:
in order A the block after the second rest is force pilot, in order
B it is reaction. Both are reported, and the reaction anchor's
behaviour in the two cells is the check on it (Section 4.6, L8).

A rest before a block changes what the block means. When you stop
and start again, the first trials after the break are often better
than the last trials before it. Bonstrup et al (2019, Current
Biology) called that micro-offline gain and read it as rapid
consolidation; Gupta and Rickard (2022, npj Science of Learning,
N = 159) reproduced the same post-rest jumps from a model with no
offline learning at all, in which inhibition builds while you work
and dissipates while you rest; Das et al (2025, PNAS) showed the
gains disappear within seconds under equal testing conditions and
appear for random sequences that cannot be learned. The two camps
disagree about the mechanism and agree about the thing this design
has to handle: a block straight after a rest starts fast. So the
rests sit at the same two places for everybody in a cell, their real
length is logged, and the analysis reports the first-trials-after-
rest against last-trials-before-rest contrast separately (Section
4.5) so the thesis can say how much of a pre-post gain is a
post-rest jump. Rest LENGTH is not a worry: 15 s, 30 s and
self-paced inter-block rests gave identical implicit sequence
learning at n = 268 (Szucs-Bencze et al 2023, eNeuro).

Fatigue over 90 minutes. Sustained-attention performance declines
within the first half hour and a proper rest restores more than a
task switch does (Helton and Russell 2015, Cognition). Micro-breaks
of ten minutes or less reduce fatigue and raise vigour across 22
studies (Albulescu et al 2022, PLOS ONE). Long cognitive test
sessions raise felt fatigue without lowering scores (Ackerman and
Kanfer 2009, JEP Applied, 3.5 to 5.5 h, N = 239), so 90 minutes of
short varied games with two rests is well inside what students
tolerate. Muscle fatigue is the one to avoid: training at or beyond
fatigue impaired skill acquisition on later days even after the
fatigue had gone (Branscheidt et al 2019, eLife). Hence: no block
over about 7 minutes, force targets at a fraction of the
participant's own maximum, the force mode's own rests left in, two
rests placed so no stretch of continuous play exceeds about 33
minutes, and the two 0 to 10 tiredness questions on the paper sheet
(Section 5.2).

2.4 Short forms and frozen ladders: exact config keys

Implemented: the battery is a protocol preset in config/default.yaml
(protocol.presets.study_battery, id healthy_single_session_v1). The

READY countdown and its own rests; the session strip reads PLAY ALL

No --config file and no per-participant yaml is needed; a study
laptop only needs session.suggest_code: always so the login offers
P01 on an empty tree (the default, auto, offers a code once one
exists on the machine).

The two waits are preset keys, not code: stretch_s 60 (a suggestion,
the button is never locked), rest_s 180 and rest_min_s 60 (the
button is held for the first minute, then reads "Start early"). An
entry carrying rest_before wins over one carrying stretch_before, so
a card never shows two countdowns.

The keys the preset applies, with the values the design fixed
(tests/test_study_battery.py pins this list exactly: nothing outside
it is touched, so a scoring, window or detection key can never creep
in through the preset):

  reaction:
    sub_mode: choice
    block_trials: 20              # 25 in the two-visit design
    response_windows_s: [2.0]     # one rung: window never moves
  force_pilot:
    passes: 1                     # one climb of the fixed ladder
  chords:
    subblocks: 3                  # within, within, cross
    trials_per_subblock: 20
    sync_windows_ms: [150]        # one rung: W fixed at 150 ms
  buzz_hunt:

    distractor_trials_per_hand: 0
    span_trials: 4                # 8 in the two-visit design
    gap_trials_per_hand: 0
    catch_rate: 0.1
  pattern:
    short_session: true
    soc_cycles_per_block: 3
    random_block_trials: 32
  echo:
    games: 2                      # best of two, and their spread
    max_len: 10                   # ceiling, to hold the 3 min line
  syllables:
    rung: 3                       # the mid rung, played once
    words_per_block: 12
    round_size: 6
    break_s: 10
  game:
    total_trials: 40              # pins the adaptive block length

  mirror: defaults.

Every one of those is a COUNT, a rest or a frozen ladder rung. None
of them moves a threshold, a window or a scoring rule, which is what
makes the PRE block and the POST block the same task.

Everything else runs on the shipped config/default.yaml as it is,
with config/user_settings.yaml left at its defaults on the study
laptop: the cue mix (buzz and sound before the target, nothing
after, target shown), test mode off, balanced targets, reports on,
EEG off, quick calibration on, ports auto-assigned in plug order
(RIGHT board first). The preset does not write these; the run sheet
checks them and every block's config_snapshot records them. The
intake fields the software does not hold (affected side, impairment
score) stay empty for a study code; the cohort is identified by the
code prefix and by battery.id healthy_single_session_v1 in every
battery block's metadata, and "healthy control" is written on the
intake sheet, not into metadata.json.

Two exceptions to "the two passes are identical", both deliberate
and both reported. Buzz Hunt's response-window ladder moves inside a
block and resets at the next block, so PRE and POST both start at
the 3.0 s window and "top level reached" is comparable between them.
Echo's Hebb sequence and Buzz Hunt's Hebb span are seeded from the
participant code, so the POST block re-presents the same repeating
sequence: that is extra exposure, and the analysis reports
Hebb-minus-novel accuracy in PRE and POST separately rather than
pooling them.

Ladder freezes were checked against the code: force_pilot climbs its
fixed 12-level wave ladder once per pass; chords and reaction take
the window ladder as a list and clamp the level to its length, so a
one-entry list is a fixed window. Each is covered by a regression
test through the real engine (tests/test_study_battery.py,
ShortFormTests).

Statistical cost of each short form:

  Force Pilot, one pass of the ladder, 24 runs over both hands.
  Per-finger metrics rest on a single run per level, so per-finger
  ICCs are not estimable with useful precision and get no MDC; the
  hand-level mean is the unit. Played twice, so the pre-post change
  has 28 pairs.

  Chords, 60 chords per pass. About 20 within-hand chords per hand
  per pass across 11 chord types, so per-chord cells are
  cohort-pooled; the start and end enslaving matrices are sub-block
  1 and sub-block 2, so the start-to-end fatigue read inside a block
  is weak (the PRE-to-POST read replaces it).

  Buzz Hunt, 12 localisation trials per hand and 4 span trials per
  pass, no gap or distractor stage. The window ladder promotes on
  six correct of the last eight, so 12 trials allow at most one
  promotion per hand and the top level is censored; the notebook
  reports the level distribution rather than a mean. Three
  localisation trials per finger per pass, so the confusion matrix
  is cohort-pooled.

  Pattern, 36-press takes and a 32-trial random take, in both
  passes. The first cycle of every take is trimmed by the mode's RT
  hygiene, leaving 24 analysable presses per take against 48 in the
  full form, so the probe-minus-flanker standard error grows by
  about 1.4. Sequence exposure carries from the PRE block into the
  POST block for the same code (the sequence is seeded from the
  code), so the POST block's trained-take RT is the retention read
  after a filled 40 minute delay. That is a WITHIN-session retention
  probe and not a substitute for a next-day test.

  Reaction, 20 trials per hand per pass, window fixed at 2.0 s. The
  fixed window costs nothing (a healthy adult almost never exceeds
  1.2 s). The trial count does: a split-half reliability inside a
  20-trial block is estimated from two halves of ten, which is why
  the notebook uses a permutation split-half over 1000 random splits
  and prints its interval rather than a single Spearman-Brown
  number.

  Syllables and adaptive, one block each. Descriptive only: no
  reliability, no pre-post, no known-effect check. They are in the
  session so every mode is played and so the middle of the sitting
  changes pace, which is itself a break from the repeated material.

4.3 Descriptives and normative ranges
-------------------------------------
Per metric and hand_role, phase pre: n, mean, SD, median, IQR, 5th
and 95th percentiles. The PRE table is the normative table the
thesis prints, because it is the one taken on a fresh hand. The POST
table is printed beside it, kept separate, and is not pooled with
it. Distribution plots per metric, with the known-effect reference
(for example Kessels 6.2 for span) drawn as a line where one
exists.

4.4 Dominant versus non-dominant
--------------------------------
Per metric with a hand_role pair, phase pre (phase post as
replication):
paired difference (dominant minus non-dominant), 95 percent CI,
paired t, Cohen dz = mean difference / SD of differences with a
bootstrap CI (10000 resamples, seeded, the notebook already has
BOOT_N and BOOT_SEED), Shapiro-Wilk on the differences; where the
differences are non-normal or n under 20 for a metric, the Wilcoxon
signed-rank test with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation
as the effect size. Report both when they disagree. Directional
hypotheses from Section 1 (R2, M2) are one-sided; everything else
two-sided with no claim beyond the CI.

4.5 Within-session reliability, SEM, MDC and change
---------------------------------------------------
With one sitting there is no between-visit ICC, so reliability is
measured twice over, at two different time scales, and both are
labelled for what they are.

(a) Split-half inside a block. For every metric that can be
recomputed from a subset of the block's trials, split the block's
scorable trials into two halves at random, compute the metric on
each half, correlate the halves across participants, and
Spearman-Brown correct. Do it over 1000 random splits (seeded) and
report the median and the 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles rather than
one odd-even number: with 20 reaction trials a single split is
noisy, and the permutation interval says how noisy. This is INTERNAL
CONSISTENCY, the tightest possible bound: nothing at all changes
between the two halves.

(b) Block to block, PRE against POST. ICC(3,1) (two-way mixed,
consistency, single measure) and ICC(2,1) (two-way random, absolute
agreement) via icc_two_one, with the exact F-based 95 percent CI
(McGraw and Wong 1996; Shrout and Fleiss 1979 forms 3,1 and 2,1).
The gap between them is the size of the uniform shift, which in this
design is the within-session change itself. Printed with the words
"within-session, 40 minutes apart, same sitting" attached, because a
reader who sees an ICC and assumes test-retest will overstate it.

SEM = SD_pooled x sqrt(1 - ICC(2,1)), SD_pooled over both blocks.
MDC95 = 1.96 x sqrt(2) x SEM (Weir 2005, J Strength Cond Res
19:231-240), reported also as a fraction of the PRE SD so a reader
sees whether the device can detect a change smaller than the normal
spread. Every one of these is a WITHIN-SESSION MDC and is named that
way in the table, the figure captions and the thesis text. The
notebook exports the per-metric MDC95 to
sessions/cohort_results/progress_mdc.yaml, which is the file that
lets the app's TODAY panel say "beyond noise" instead of just
"different"; until a cohort exists the app shows direction and a
number and never the word "real".

(c) The change itself, PRE to POST. Per metric and hand_role: the
paired difference with a 95 percent CI, paired t, Cohen dz with a
bootstrap CI (10000 resamples, seeded), Shapiro-Wilk on the
differences, and Wilcoxon with the matched-pairs rank-biserial where
the differences are non-normal. Plus a RESPONDER COUNT: how many
participants moved further than the metric's own within-session
MDC95, in the better direction, with a Wilson interval on the
proportion. The responder count is the number a clinician
understands and the one the app's screen is a per-person version of.

(d) Bland-Altman on the same pair: bias (post minus pre) with 95
percent CI and limits of agreement, per metric.

(e) The post-rest contrast. Two of the POST blocks start straight
after a 3 minute rest, and a block that starts after a rest starts
fast for reasons that are argued over and are not learning (Section
2.2). For every mode whose fine series is per trial, compare the
first three scorable trials of the POST block against the last three
of the PRE block, separately from the whole-block difference, and
report it beside the pre-post change so the thesis can say how much
of the gain arrived in the break. The reaction block never follows a
rest in order A and does in order B, which is the between-cell check
(L8).

(f) Learning slopes. Per participant and metric, fit the fine series
(Section 4.7 figure 5) with a log-linear model (value against
log(trial index)) and report the slope, its CI and the cohort
distribution; where a series has at least 8 points, also fit an
exponential (asymptote plus amplitude times exp(-rate x index)) and
report the rate. A power law and an exponential are hard to tell
apart in averaged data, and averaging across people can manufacture
a smooth power curve out of individually exponential ones (Heathcote,
Brown and Mewhort 2000, Psychonomic Bulletin and Review), so the
fits are PER PERSON and the cohort figure is a distribution of
slopes, never a curve fitted to the group mean.

Metrics flagged in Section 1 as poor by construction (pattern
learning score, buzz hunt accuracy at ceiling, the censored window
level) get their ICC printed with the reason and no MDC, and are
excluded from the responder count.

4.6 Known-effect and learning checks (pre-specified)
----------------------------------------------------
Two tables, both decided before the data comes in.

Table 1, known effects, one row per check, from Section 1: R1 R2 R3,
P1 P2 P3, C1 C2 C3 C4, Rh1 Rh2, M1 M2, F1 F2 F3 F4, B1 B2 B3 B4,
E1 E2 E3. Each row: statistic, CI, test, effect size, pass or fail
against the stated criterion. Every one of them is now computed on
the PRE blocks (the fresh-hand measurement); the ones that can be
repeated are computed again on the POST blocks and reported as a
replication, not pooled. Two need a note in the new design: R3 (no
practice effect in reaction) becomes a within-session claim, and P2
(pattern probe rebound) is now measured in both blocks with the
POST block also giving the retention read.

Table 2, learning checks, pre-registered with a direction and a
rough size from Section 4.5's literature and the mode docstrings:

  L1 reaction    median_rt_ms stable within +/- 20 ms pre to post.
                 Tested as EQUIVALENCE (two one-sided tests against
                 a +/- 20 ms band), not as a failure to reject: "we
                 found no difference" is not evidence of no
                 difference. This is the anchor, and a fail here
                 means the whole session's pre-post results are
                 contaminated by fatigue or drift.
  L2 mirror      mean_gap_ms down 10 to 20 percent.
  L3 rhythm      asyn_sd_ms down a few ms; asyn_mean_ms stays
                 negative (Repp 2005).
  L4 echo        span 0 to +1 (Calamia 2012 gives d 0.16 to 0.34 for
                 span-type retests); hebb_minus_novel_acc up.
  L5 force_pilot mae_pct down 15 to 30 percent, most of it inside
                 the PRE block itself (Forman 2020, Zvornik 2024).
  L6 chords      median_er and median_span_ms down, clean_hit_rate
                 up, all modest.
  L7 buzz_hunt   loc_median_rt_ms down; loc_accuracy unchanged at
                 ceiling; window_top_level up.
  L8 order       the pre-post change for the block that follows
                 REST 2 differs between cell A (force pilot) and
                 cell B (reaction). Reported as a contrast with a
                 CI, not tested; n is 14 a side.

Adaptive and syllables appear in neither table: played once,
descriptive only.

This is the known-effect check of the thesis; it is construct
evidence (the game reproduces what the literature predicts), not
criterion validity against a clinical measure, which a healthy
cohort cannot give. No other inferential test is run; everything
else is descriptive with CIs.

5. ETHICS AND RUN SHEET
=======================

5.1 What the ethics application must cover
------------------------------------------
Risk level: low risk under the NHMRC National Statement (2023
update; "low risk" is research where the only foreseeable risk is
one of discomfort; NHMRC, National Statement on Ethical Conduct in
Human Research, Section 2.1). Curtin HREC low-risk pathway.

Participants: adults 18 and over from the psychology participant
pool, credit per the pool's standard rate; students of the
supervisor or the student researcher are excluded to avoid a
dependent relationship. Inclusion: normal or corrected vision,
able to sit at a laptop for an hour and a half with breaks.
Exclusion: current hand,
wrist or forearm injury or pain; diagnosed neurological or
musculoskeletal condition affecting the hands; skin condition or
open wound on the hands; known sensitivity to vibration.

Procedure, in plain words: one visit of about an hour and three
quarters, with two three-minute breaks and a stretch break in it;
hands rest flat on a 3D printed frame with a small force pad
under each fingertip; short games on a laptop screen; light presses
only; small vibration motors buzz a fingertip for a quarter of a
second as a cue; a ruler measurement of each hand; four questions on
hand preference; age and sex.

Foreseeable discomforts and their controls:
  Vibration: eccentric-rotating-mass motors at fixed drive, 250 ms
    bursts, felt as a phone-style buzz. No heat, no electrical
    contact with the skin (the motor is inside the frame). Anyone
    who finds it unpleasant can stop; buzz_before can be turned off
    for that participant and the change is logged in cue_flags and
    the intake sheet.
  Sustained light pressing: targets are at most 40 percent of the
    participant's own comfortable maximum press, holds are 16 s,
    runs under 30 s, with rests built in and every rest skippable
    only by the participant's choice. Mild finger fatigue is the
    expected worst case.
  Skin contact: pads and frame wiped with 70 percent isopropyl
    between participants; participants wash or sanitise hands on
    arrival; disposable finger cots offered.
  Electrical safety: the boards run at 5 V from the laptop USB
    port; no mains-powered part touches the participant.
  Screen and sound: 60 Hz display with no flashing patterns; laptop
    speaker at a moderate fixed volume set once and recorded.
  Boredom or frustration: games are short and scored; participants
    may skip a block or withdraw at any time without loss of credit.

Data: participant code only in every file the software writes;
age, sex, handedness and hand size on the intake sheet keyed by
code; the consent log is the only link between name and code and
is destroyed at study close. Recorded data are trial tables, 200 Hz
force traces and configuration snapshots; none is identifying.
Storage on Curtin-approved storage, not personal cloud folders;
retention per Curtin's research data policy; the software
repository is public on GitHub and holds no participant data
(sessions/ is gitignored).
Participants may withdraw their data until analysis begins.

Also state: no deception beyond the standard "some games repeat a
hidden pattern you are not told about" (pattern, buzz hunt, echo),
disclosed in the debrief; no clinical assessment or feedback is
given; the device is a research prototype, not a medical device.

5.2 Research-assistant run sheet (one session)
----------------------------------------------
BEFORE (10 min)
  [ ] Laptop on mains power, app launched as shipped; the login
      screen suggests the next free code (Pxx).
  [ ] Plug the RIGHT board first, then the LEFT; login screen names
      the ports right and left. Boards self-test buzz once each.
  [ ] Pads and frame wiped. Finger cots, ruler, intake sheet, EHI
      form on the desk. Speaker at the study volume. Water on the
      desk for the rests.
  [ ] Intake sheet: date, cell (A/B, hand first), commit hash,
      session start time, caffeine in the last two hours (yes/no),
      hours of sleep last night.

ARRIVAL (15 min, off the clock)
  [ ] Consent, EHI short form, hand length and breadth both hands,
      code assigned.
  [ ] Hands washed. Seat height so forearms rest flat, fingers
      index to little on the four pads, thumbs off the frame.
  [ ] Say once: "This runs about an hour and a half with two proper
      breaks. Press lightly, like typing. Some games have a hidden
      rule; do not try to work it out, just play." Never mention
      sequences or patterns again.
  [ ] Say once, and only once: "You will play everything twice, at
      the start and near the end. The screen will show you how the
      second go compared with the first." Do not repeat it, do not
      comment on the numbers during the session, and do not
      encourage between blocks: the whole pre-post read depends on
      the RA behaving identically in both passes.

CLOCK STARTS (target 85 min, hard stop 90)
  [ ] Check the login: code, age, sex, MAIN HAND (Left or Right,
      required), hand length and breadth. The EHI score stays on the
      sheet. LOG IN. Quick calibration runs for both hands: hands
      off, hands resting, then a light press per finger. If a finger
      fails, redo once, then continue and note it.
  [ ] Press PLAY ALL on the hub (or A). The app plays the cell's
      order below; after each block press Start on the NEXT UP card
      (or N). The card names the step, the hand and the wait; the
      strip reads PLAY ALL k/20 with the minutes beside it, amber
      past 85 and red past 90. A block that cannot be run (a board
      dropped) is skipped from the hub with Skip step (S) and the
      reason written on the sheet; a block ended early is offered
      again, Retry redoes it.

  Order A                          Order B
   1 Reaction, hand 1               1 Force Pilot, both
   2 Reaction, hand 2               2 Chords, both
   3 Mirror, both                   3 Buzz Hunt, both
   4 Rhythm, both, Easy_Lemon,      4 Reaction, hand 1
     Medium                           (60 s stretch)
   5 Echo, both                     5 Reaction, hand 2
     (60 s stretch)                 6 Mirror, both
   6 Force Pilot, both              7 Rhythm, both, Easy_Lemon,
   7 Chords, both                     Medium
   8 Buzz Hunt, both                8 Echo, both
   9 Pattern, dominant hand         9 Pattern, dominant hand
     (REST 1, 3 min)                  (REST 1, 3 min)
  10 Adaptive, dominant hand       10 Adaptive, dominant hand
  11 Syllables, both               11 Syllables, both
  12 to 20: blocks 1 to 9 again    12 to 20: blocks 1 to 9 again,
     in the same order, with          in the same order, with
     REST 2 before block 17           REST 2 before block 15

  Hand 1 is the dominant hand for codes ending 1 and 2 in each
  group of four (P01, P02, P05, P06, ...), the non-dominant hand
  for P03, P04, P07, P08, ...

  Per block: read the on-screen card, confirm the participant is
  ready, start. Do not coach during a block. Let the participant
  skip a mode's own internal rests with Space only when they say
  they are ready. Force Pilot and Buzz Hunt refuse to start without
  the boards; if a board has dropped, reconnect and note the block.
  [ ] Tick each block on the sheet as it ends; write the reason for
      any early end or skip.

  AT EACH REST (2 x 3 min) AND AT THE END
  [ ] Hands off the pads. Offer water. Do not talk about the games,
      the numbers or how it is going.
  [ ] Ask the two questions and write the answers on the sheet,
      exactly as worded, 0 = not at all, 10 = completely:
        "Tired in the head?"   0 to 10
        "Tired in the hands?"  0 to 10
      Three sets of answers per session (REST 1, REST 2, end). Note
      the clock time next to each.
  [ ] The Start button is held for the first minute of a rest and
      then reads "Start early". Take the full three minutes unless
      the participant asks to go on; write down when a rest was cut
      short.

  [ ] At 90 minutes: let the current block finish, stop, record what
      was missed. The POST pattern block is the one to lose.

END
  [ ] The last block's screen shows the TODAY table. Let the
      participant read it. Answer questions about their own numbers
      only; say plainly that a change smaller than the game's noise
      is not a real change, and that no comparison with other
      people is being shown or made.
  [ ] End the session from the hub (confirm the session-end
      warning). Check sessions/<today>/ holds one folder per block
      played, each with trials.csv, raw.csv, metadata.json, and that
      the phase words read pre, mid, post in order.
  [ ] Debrief: hidden sequences explained, the point of playing
      everything twice explained, questions answered, credit
      granted, thanks. Say that one session shows change during a
      sitting and does not show what is kept overnight.
  [ ] Copy today's session folders to the approved storage; wipe
      pads and frame; unplug boards.
